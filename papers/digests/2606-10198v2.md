## Summary

This paper genuinely estimates a one-dimensional density ridge. Each completed hidden-
state trajectory is reduced to six handcrafted kinematic coordinates; a Gaussian KDE is
fit on labelled-correct trajectories and SCMS extracts a 1-D ridge. Confidence is the
negative distance to the ridge, averaged across five generations.

The estimator is geometrically substantive, but correctness enters through correct-only
calibration and a stated both-class supervised projection front-end. The evaluation is a
small, selectively reported extended abstract with unresolved protocol inconsistencies.
It is not evidence that density ridges on our DSP features solve target identification.

## Geometry, supervision, and access contract

- **Geometric object:** final-layer hidden-state trajectory over autoregressive tokens.
- **Feature map:** mean/peak curvature, displacement linearity, mean/peak displacement,
  and normalized position of maximum curvature; one point in \(\mathbb R^6\) per generation.
- **Actual geometry:** Gaussian KDE, gradient/Hessian SCMS, a one-dimensional ridge,
  and LTSA/arclength/atlas coordinate variants.
- **Supervision:** correctness labels define the correct-only KDE sample; the limitation
  section additionally states that the projection front-end needs both classes.
- **Access:** white-box hidden trajectories and five sampled completions per query.
- **Deployment:** retrospective completed-query score; not causal because curvature,
  peak position, and all five completed trajectories are required.
- **Calibration:** 200 queries with five generations; reported test size is only 60.

## Datasets & models used

Named benchmarks are HaluEval-QA, TriviaQA, GSM8K, POPE, ScienceQA, and A-OKVQA.
Named models include Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Gemma-2-9B-IT, LLaVA-1.5-7B,
Idefics3-8B-Llama3, and SmolVLM-Instruct. The abstract says eight models/six
benchmarks while the contributions say nine/seven; a complete roster is absent.

## Methods it compared itself against

Sequence/mean log probability, Semantic Entropy, TRACED mean curvature, kNN/PCA/
Mahalanobis geometry, correct-centroid flow matching, LID-MLE, and persistent homology.
Eleven SCMS configurations and three naive-geometry variants are mentioned, but their
full outcome table and selection protocol are not shown.

## Calibration, leakage, and failure cases

- Queries are retained only if they generate an ambiguously written “at least >=3–6”
  correct samples, inconsistent with the stated five-generation budget. This creates
  label-conditioned selection and leaves the eligible population unclear.
- Different cells report different ridge coordinate and ablation variants; no single
  frozen finalist or nested selection protocol is described.
- No CV, seeds, grouped split, CIs, code, or data release is specified.
- Near-perfect AUROCs are reported on 60 test queries after a wide variant sweep.
- The theorem assumes incorrect samples lie a positive distance from the correct ridge;
  it does not derive that target separation from unlabeled geometry.
- No no-label, single-generation, causal-prefix, transfer, or first-error ablation exists.

## Connection to our pipeline

A direct DSP adaptation would fit

\[
\widehat R^+=\operatorname{SCMS}\!\left(\widehat p(X\mid Y=\mathrm{correct})\right),
\qquad
a_i=\operatorname{dist}(X_i,\widehat R^+).
\]

The superscript “+” is the entire target anchor. Without labels, SCMS only estimates
a ridge of \(P(X)\), reproducing the failure class already observed in Atomic Operator,
A4/A5, DSP-contextual IU, and broad-28 DUFS. A supervised ridge on our features would
be a nonlinear ceiling and must beat a capacity-matched global LR—not IU or a family
baseline—to show incremental value.

The original hidden-state kinematics are genuinely new telemetry, but obtaining them
changes the access tier and requires new inference. The paper supplies no credible
Localization or Early evidence.

## Recommendation

**Reject for implementation; diagnostic only as a literature hypothesis.** The evidence
quality is insufficient to justify hidden-state/repeated-generation inference, and a DSP
ridge would not solve target identifiability.
