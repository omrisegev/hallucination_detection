## Summary

CROP (Conformal Reasoning Output Prefixes) is a split-conformal / conformal-risk-control layer on top of any
fixed step-level risk proxy. On a labeled calibration split it picks the largest threshold λ̂ from a
predeclared grid such that (1 + #calibration traces whose retained prefix contains an annotated error)/(n+1)
≤ α. At test time it returns the longest prefix whose step risks are all ≤ λ̂ (first-crossing rule) and
routes the suffix to review or repair. Guarantee (Prop. 1, p.6): finite-sample **marginal**
P(returned prefix contains an annotated error) ≤ α under reasoning-instance exchangeability, relative to the
fixed annotation protocol. The paper states it is not conditional per prefix. The proxies are Random,
Token/format LR, Trace-feature LR (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), Direct PRM (Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B) and a supervised
Trace+PRM LR. The authors argue step AUROC is an incomplete guide to prefix utility (Fig. 4, Prop. 2).

## Datasets & models used

- CRV [8] Arithmetic (1,500 traces / 10,316 steps / 3.4% error steps) and GSM8K (1,319 / 8,995 / 7.3%).
  These are the main benchmark, with dense labels from automatic checks or a stronger-model judge, and traces
  generated by a Llama-family model.
- ProcessBench (3,400 / 25,697 / 6.6%), Math-Shepherd (10,000 / 37,514 / 47.6%), PRMBench (5,000 / 67,445 /
  15.9%; 96.4% of held-out traces have an error, p.28) and PRM800K (8,000 / 70,698 / 4.2%). Table 4, p.21.
- Models: frozen Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B (risk = 1 − softmax(class logits at `<extra_0>`)_correct, one pass,
  truncation at 8192 tokens). Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct for the trace features, teacher-forced on the written step
  text ("score the already-written step text", p.20). Repair models: Gemma 4 E4B IT, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct,
  DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B and Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, via Ollama with deterministic decoding.

## Methods it compared itself against

- Interfaces: question-only input, full-trace input, and **conformal whole-trace abstention**, calibrated
  separately at the same α.
- Risk proxies compared against each other: Random, Token/format, Trace features, Direct PRM, Trace+PRM.
- No other step-level calibration method and no label-free fusion baseline.

## Experiments — methodology & scores

- Splits are 60/20/20 at the trace level (proxy fit / calibration / test), stratified by domain and by
  has-error. Seeds 2806–2825 (20) for CRV and 2806–2815 (10) for the four external sets.
- Grids: for CRV, 201 quantiles of the training-split step scores; for the external sets (PRMBench included),
  a fixed {0, 0.01, …, 1}. α = 0.05 is primary; 0.025 and 0.10 are also reported.
- The LRs use median imputation, StandardScaler, ℓ2 penalty, C = 1, L-BFGS, max_iter 500, balanced class
  weights and no post-hoc calibration. They are fitted on pooled training steps with Y_t; nothing is fitted
  per answer.
- Metrics:
  - pooled step AUROC;
  - retained-prefix fraction (mean M/T);
  - prefix-contamination risk (% of test traces);
  - boundary deviation |M − O|/T, split into over-withholding and unsafe overshoot, with O the first-error
    index;
  - repair exact-match accuracy.

## Connection to our pipeline

**Inputs.**
- CROP stated:
  - surface/position counts;
  - Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct teacher-forced summaries of token confidence, sequence likelihood, entropy,
    "calibrated confidence" and energy-style uncertainty;
  - hidden-state trajectory scalars (CoE [56], CoT-Kinetics [57]).
- That is a second model, separate from the PRM, and partly white-box.
- Ours: telemetry of the PRM's own backbone from the same forward pass, gray-box only.
- Conceptual overlap (inference): surprisal, entropy, energy. Unique to ours: turnover, JS, spectral
  frequency, BOCPD, innovations, digit channels. Unique to theirs: hidden-state trajectories and surface cues.
- The exact CROP feature list and its token→step aggregation are not in the PDF.

**Fusion.**
- CROP: supervised pooled LR on step labels.
- Ours: label-free, transductive L-SML on answer-standardized channels.

**Decision rule.**
- CROP: one global λ, calibrated with labels, with a prefix guarantee.
- Ours: per-answer GAP flag count, label-free, with no guarantee.

**Evaluation.**
- CROP: pooled AUROC plus prefix kept/risk. It reports no PRMScore and no within-answer AUC.

**Our CPU parity** (`results/prmbench_paper_metric_parity_v1/README.md` §2, an approximate subset):
- Our Direct PRM reproduces their row (.7999 / 4.8 / 0.6). On a quantile grid the same PRM keeps 17.2%.
- Label-free fused arms keep 23.0–25.1% at risk ≤ 5%, vs 21.8% for Trace+PRM. They are on a different grid,
  and the fusion was fitted transductively.
- A supervised LR on our telemetry plus the PRM keeps 24.8% (fixed grid).

**Adoptable (inference).**
- A CROP calibration layer on top of our label-free score: a certificate from a small labeled calibration set.
- CRC for a monotone per-answer flag rule.
- Prefix utility, boundary deviation, artifact-matched summaries, and label/order-shuffle controls as report
  rows.

## Notes / open questions

- **Direct PRM on PRMBench (4.8% kept, 0.6% risk) is mostly a grid artifact.** The fixed 0.01 grid is too
  coarse near risk 0, so the feasible λ is 0 (our parity check). Math-Shepherd Direct PRM (1.0% kept,
  0.9% risk) likely has the same issue (inference).
- **Trace+PRM's gain over Direct PRM on PRMBench is unattributed.** No Token/format+PRM row exists, and
  Trace features ≈ Token/format on PRMBench and ProcessBench. The +.033 AUROC may come from surface/position
  cues rather than model features (inference).
- **Feature source is ambiguous.** p.20 says the features come from "the trace-generating language model";
  p.9 says Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. For the external datasets the generator is not Llama-3.1-8B.
- **Which 5,000 PRMBench rows** is not stated in the PDF. From their code: the first 5,000 of
  `hitsmy/PRMBench_Preview`.
- **Seed count inconsistency.** Table 5 mentions "50 target splits"; the text says 20 seeds.
- **Possible leakage.** Math-Shepherd AUROC .99 (trace features) and PRM800K .99 (token/format) suggest
  label leakage through format or position. The authors do not discuss this.
