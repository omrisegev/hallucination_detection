## Summary

This is not a hallucination-detection paper. It studies neural/CNN object-category
manifolds and derives a target-aware decomposition of global linear-regression error
into within-category error and a local-to-global gap. The gap is further attributed
to centroid, scale, and orientation differences between category-specific regressors.

Its useful lesson for this project is conditional: coarse representational similarity
can remain very high while the target-specific readout geometry changes materially.
That is a strong warning against treating a stable manifold as target identification,
but the paper itself obtains the relevant direction from supervised regression labels.

## Geometry, supervision, and access contract

- **Geometric object:** activations elicited by images of the same object category;
  each category is a manifold in the CNN/neural population space.
- **Actual geometry:** category centroids, within-manifold covariance/PC spectra,
  participation ratio, alignment, and category-local regression vectors. This is a
  substantive manifold analysis, not merely geometric language.
- **Target:** four known continuous bounding-box coordinates, plus object category.
- **Supervision:** explicit category and coordinate labels train the CNN heads and
  define every local regression vector. The decisive geometry is target-supervised.
- **Access:** white-box 2,048-dimensional ResNet activations and macaque neural
  responses; not gray-box logit telemetry.
- **Sampling/online status:** large offline population analysis; neither single-pass
  deployment nor causal/online detection applies.
- **Calibration/leakage:** decoder data are separated from network training and ridge
  hyperparameters use CV. No material leakage issue was apparent for the paper's task.

## Datasets & models used

- A generated photorealistic dataset with 265 object categories and 20,000 images per
  category: 10,000 for training and 10,000 for representation/decoder analysis.
- Image generation uses Objects365 seeds, CerberusDet, SDXL/SD1.5-based processing.
- ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 variants: classification-only (C), regression-only
  (R), joint classification/regression (CR), and category-local regression (CRloc).
- Public macaque V4/IT recordings from the earlier joint-code study.

## Methods it compared itself against

The main comparison is factorial across training objectives rather than a detector
leaderboard: C versus R versus CR versus CRloc, along with layer, unit-count,
category-count, and training-schedule ablations. Linear category decoders and global
and category-local ridge regressors are evaluated on held-out representation data.

## Experiments — methodology & scores

| Setup | Metric | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| ResNet joint code | Task performance | CR matches C classification and R regression | Shows joint coding is possible under supervision |
| C versus CR | CKA | about 0.95 | Coarse similarity remains high despite target-specific changes |
| C versus CR | Mean-manifold spectral distance | about 0.083 | Global shape changes are small |
| Regression-only R | Effective manifold dimension | about 4 | Close to the number of supervised coordinates |
| Macaque V4/IT | root-nMSE | above 0.5 | Absolute regression remains limited |

The main theoretical identity is

\[
E_{\mathrm{global}}=E_{\mathrm{local}}+\Delta E,
\]

where \(\Delta E\) measures the cost of replacing category-specific readouts by one
global readout. The paper decomposes this gap into centroid, scale, and local-direction
orientation terms. Here “orientation” means alignment of supervised local regressors,
not the sign convention of IU features.

## Ablations, failure cases, and claim strength

- CRloc can substantially reduce the local-global gap without explicitly imposing one
  shared readout. A small gap is therefore not sufficient evidence for a genuinely
  category-independent code.
- Unit and category subsampling can make distinct codes appear similar; below roughly
  200 units, the paper's controlled networks become difficult to distinguish.
- Coarse CKA and average-manifold measures miss target-specific readout differences.
- The theory assumes structured target directions, isotropic irrelevant directions,
  large sample/category regimes, and specific independence/SNR conditions.
- Claims are supported by controlled CNN experiments and analytic derivations, but the
  paper is a bioRxiv preprint and does not test LLMs or hallucinations.

## Code and data

The paper links `github.com/tiberilor/manifolds-joint-code` for data generation,
training, decoding, and theory reproduction. A separate full-dataset release was not

## Connection to our pipeline

The paper supplies no new information beyond \(P(X)\) for identifying correctness.
It instead supplies a useful audit **after** a target direction exists. If A6/PTNI
produces verified intervention-derived directions \(w_e\) in multiple environments,
we can ask whether one global affine correction is justified by decomposing

\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{transport}}
=E(w_{\mathrm{global}})-\frac{1}{|\mathcal E|}
  \sum_e E(w_e)
\]

into environment-centroid, scale, and direction-alignment terms with shrinkage.
That could diagnose whether failure is a bad global readout or absent target signal.
It cannot generate the target direction, route a new sample to an expert, or replace
IU-PCR by itself.

## Recommendation

**Diagnostic only, conditional on A6/PTNI.** Use it to audit transport and global-versus-
local target directions after a verified target contrast exists. Do not open a standalone
fusion or router experiment from this paper.
