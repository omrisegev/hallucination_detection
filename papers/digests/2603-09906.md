# Thinking to Recall: How Reasoning Unlocks Parametric Knowledge in LLMs

## Source

- arXiv:2603.09906, submitted 2026-03-10.
- Official record: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09906
- Official PDF reviewed online: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.09906
- Local PDF status: not archived in this worktree at digest time.

## Research question

The paper asks why allowing a language model to reason before answering can improve closed-book factual recall. It is not a hallucination-localization or first-error-localization paper.

The proposed explanation has two components:

1. A content-independent compute buffer: generating additional tokens can improve recall even when those tokens do not themselves supply the answer.
2. Factual priming or generative self-retrieval: intermediate generations can surface relevant parametric facts that make the final answer more likely.

## Experimental setting

- Tasks: SimpleQA and EntityQuestions closed-book factual question answering.
- Models include Gemini 2.5 Flash, Gemini 2.5 Pro, and Qwen3-32B.
- The main analysis samples many reasoning traces per question, commonly 100.
- Intermediate factual claims are extracted and checked using an external search-enabled Gemini verifier.
- Some selection analyses choose among multiple sampled traces according to extracted-fact properties.

This access pattern is materially stronger than the repository's primary single-pass gray-box ProcessBench/PRMBench lane.

## Main empirical observations

- Extra reasoning tokens can improve factual recall even when the generated content is not directly relevant, supporting a compute-buffer contribution.
- Traces that surface useful factual content are more likely to end in a correct answer, supporting a factual-priming contribution.
- Intermediate factual hallucinations correlate with worse final answers. The reported aggregate answer accuracies for clean versus hallucinated traces are approximately 41.4 versus 26.4 percent on SimpleQA and 71.1 versus 32.2 percent on EntityQuestions.
- The paper also performs within-question analyses; their slopes remain below one, which helps separate the trace association from question-difficulty confounding.
- Selecting traces with extracted or verified factual content can improve expected answer accuracy, but this depends on multiple samples, fact extraction, and external verification.

## What transfers to reasoning localization

### Useful diagnostic ideas

1. Factual-claim stratification

   Tag whether a ProcessBench trace or step contains factual claims and report localizer behavior by that tag. This can test whether entropy dynamics localize mathematical/procedural errors differently from false factual premises.

2. Within-question paired analysis

   When multiple scorer copies or samples exist for a source question, compare traces within the question. This controls for question difficulty and complements the repository's requirement to group source copies in folds and bootstraps.

3. Length and compute as nuisance controls

   Report trace length or compute budget as a covariate and stratifier. The paper's length effect is not simply monotonic, so length must not be treated as correctness evidence or included in a compact risk vector without a separate justification.

4. High-access semantic ceiling

   A separate ceiling could use an external verifier to mark unsupported intermediate factual claims, then measure how well that oracle-like signal localizes the first error. It must be named as a multi-sample/external-verifier ceiling and cannot compete in the same-access primary table.

## What does not transfer

- The paper does not evaluate ProcessBench, PRMBench, exact first-error F1, clean abstention, or early prefix detection.
- Its core evidence is closed-book factual QA rather than mathematical reasoning-error localization.
- The strongest selection procedures use many samples and an external verifier/search system.
- Factual correctness annotations are supervised semantic labels. They cannot be used to orient, fit, or select a label-free entropy localizer.
- The paper does not provide evidence for adding a generic factual-priming scalar to the compact same-access feature roster.
- It does not establish a token-to-step pooling or calibration rule transferable to the repository evaluator.

## Decision for REASONING_LOCALIZATION_03662_ANCHOR_V1

- Primary roster: no new coordinate is added from this paper.
- Phase 2: no direct candidate arm.
- Phase 4 diagnostics: add factual-claim and trace-length strata only if the tags can be produced without affecting the frozen scorer.
- Optional ceiling: external-verifier semantic localization, separately access-labeled and excluded from promotion against Mind the Gap.

The paper is therefore inspiration for confound control and a semantic ceiling, not evidence for a new compact uncertainty feature.
