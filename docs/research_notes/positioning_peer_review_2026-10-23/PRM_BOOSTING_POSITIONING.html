PRM Boosting against the closest methods

Our method fuses a frozen PRM with telemetry from the same forward pass, at the step level and without labels. Against the two papers on the same PRM and benchmark, it matches CROP's supervised proxy (22.3% vs 21.8% of each trace kept, inside the seed spread) and reaches PRISM's PRMScore without retraining. Most of the PRISM-level score comes from our flag rule; fusion adds 2.4 points on top of it. The estimator family is not new: FUSE (ICML 2026) uses the same Nadler line to ensemble verifiers, but only to select whole responses. As far as we have searched, no paper fuses at the step level.

  • Where the gain comes from. With one global threshold the fused score and the PRM trace almost the same curve. The fused score pulls ahead only when each answer is cut at its own largest gap.
  • The rule has a prior built in. GAP always flags at least one step. On the one category of correct solutions (MS), PRMScore falls from 99.87 for the native PRM to 94.64 for the PRM under GAP and 88.87 for L-SML under GAP. On PRMBench, GAP does not beat flagging a constant 2 steps per answer (−0.35), which is the benchmark's construction prior. Leaderboard PRMs use native thresholds, so a raw leaderboard comparison mixes the rule with the method.
  • On the other PRMs, at matched TPR, the fused score is ahead even under a global threshold: PURE FPR 49.8 vs 52.2, RLHFlow 57.8 vs 77.1, Math-Shepherd 57.3 vs 81.3. On Qwen-72B it is slightly behind (45.9 vs 45.3).
  • The claim we can make: without retraining and without labels, the same PRM reaches PRISM's PRMScore, and fusion adds 2.4 points over the PRM under the same rule. We should not claim we beat PRISM: at PRISM's own operating point, fusion and the plain PRM tie. PRISM's checkpoint is not public, so we cannot boost it.

CROP: the same idea with labels, matched without them

CROP's Trace+PRM proxy is the closest prior task: a step-level fusion of a PRM with model telemetry. Theirs is a logistic regression trained on step labels from 60% of the traces. We reproduced their protocol on our scores: the first 5,000 PRMBench traces (4,995 scored), their splits, seeds 2806–2815, risk ≤ 5%. CROP searches a fixed grid of 101 thresholds. We report that grid and a finer quantile grid, which keeps a little more for every proxy. We also added controls they do not have.

  • On Qwen-7B, the PRM CROP used, label-free L-SML matches their supervised proxy at the same risk: 22.3% vs 21.8% on CROP's grid, with our fusion fitted only on the training traces. The margin is about a quarter of our seed SD (1.9 points over 10 seeds), so this is a tie, not a win. Fitted on all traces, it is 21.9%. Only our variant with label-chosen channels (24.4%) clears it, and that variant is not label-free.
  • Most of CROP's gain over the PRM may come from surface cues. Our format and position features plus the PRM, with no Llama features, come within 0.6 points of their Trace+PRM on their grid (21.2% vs 21.8%; AUROC .831 vs .834). Our format/position LR alone also matches their token/format row (16.3% vs 16.2%). This is an inference from our reconstruction: we did not ablate CROP's own features.
  • On the other PRMs the picture is mixed. Against the supervised format/position + PRM LR (quantile grid, training-trace fit), L-SML is ahead on Qwen-72B (22.3% vs 21.3%) and behind on PURE (20.7 vs 22.1), RLHFlow (16.4 vs 17.8) and Math-Shepherd (17.4 vs 18.6). The order is the same on CROP's grid. The plain Qwen-72B PRM finds no threshold that meets 5% on any of the 10 seeds (risk 7.4%).
  • Our telemetry is the better input. A supervised LR on it keeps 25.1–25.4% (quantile grid, 13 or 16 channels). The label-free fusion recovers 81–92% of that on the Qwen and PURE PRMs.
  • Where CROP is stronger.
    • Its pooled AUROC is higher (.834 vs .792). Our scores are standardized within each answer, a likely reason, since pooled AUROC compares steps across answers.
    • It offers a guarantee within one domain, and ours does not survive a domain shift. Calibrated on PRMBench and tested on Socratic at a 5% target, L-SML reaches 6.7% risk on Qwen-7B and 8.2–25.9% on the other four PRMs, while each plain PRM stays at or below 5%. CROP's own cross-domain diagnostic also breaks (14.8% risk from arithmetic to GSM8K), so both need recalibration under shift.

The label-free ensembling family: FUSE and CARE

These two ICML 2026 papers use estimators close to ours. They never work at the step level, so they define the method family, not a table to beat. A reviewer will still expect them cited and run as baselines.

FUSE (Lee, Ma, Zhao, Nair, Spector, Cohen, Candès; Stanford and Google)

Builds directly on Nadler's line: Parisi et al. 2014 (SML) and Jaffe et al. 2015 and 2016 (dependent classifiers). It ensembles verifier scores for N responses to one question and returns the best response.

  • Step 1: per-verifier thresholds chosen to minimize a label-free measure of how far triplets of verifiers are from conditional independence.
  • Step 2: the Jaffe 2015 method of moments estimates each verifier's sensitivity and specificity.
  • Step 3: those estimates give pseudo-labels, used to fit an aggregator such as a logistic regression.
  • It matches Weaver, which uses labels for 5% of questions, and wins 27 of 40 comparisons against the supervised baselines.
  • Their finding mirrors ours. The plain Jaffe 2015 weighting loses to a simple average in 7 of 10 settings. On our corrected-template PRMBench banks, the plain mean matches or beats L-SML on 7 of 10 banks.

CARE (Zhao, Shin, Huang, Namburi, Sala; UW-Madison)

Aggregates LLM-judge scores while modelling shared confounders between judges. It cites Anandkumar's tensor methods and the weak-supervision literature, but not Nadler.

  • A sparse + low-rank split of the judges' precision matrix gives a dependency graph.
  • The graph picks three conditionally independent judge groups; a tensor CP decomposition (or an SVD for continuous scores) recovers judge quality.
  • Hyperparameters are tuned on a 15% labeled split.
  • It reaches up to 26.8% lower error than majority vote on 12 judge benchmarks, none of them step-level reasoning.
  • It addresses the same obstacle we have: the estimates choose channels but do not weight them better than an average.
  • What is new in our work is the level and the views, not the estimator: one answer at a time, step by step, with the views taken from the PRM's own forward pass, scored with official PRMScore.
  • What they give us: two candidate fusion rules for the open direction of replacing the plain average, both runnable on CPU from our existing scores. Neither has been run on our data yet.
  • The Ising-model aggregation paper (arXiv 2601.22336, ICML 2026) adds theory: rules that assume conditional independence stay suboptimal when judges are dependent. It works on binary votes only.

Two more neighbours

Mind the Gap (Chen, Chen, Yue, Li; ICML 2026)

A wrong chain shows a sudden fall in confidence, not low confidence on average. The signal is the negative entropy of the top-20 next-token distribution, smoothed, and its sharpest drops. One teacher-forced pass, no PRM, no fusion.

  • The score uses no labels; the accept and step thresholds are set on a labeled half of the test data.
  • ProcessBench, exact match of the first error: 32.9 vs 24.6 for average entropy on MATH with Qwen3-8B. No PRMBench.
  • Released code covers final-answer detection only and computes a different signal (top-1 log-prob).
  • Fair test: add it as a 17th channel, and run it alone on PRMBench with GAP. Not measured yet.

BR2-PRM (Chan, Zhu et al.; HKUST and PKU; ACL 2026)

A side contribution of the PRMBench-V paper. A multimodal judge scores each whole answer 1–5 on nine error types. For each type, a Dirichlet-smoothed table of scores against known labels gives P(no error | score); the answer score is the product over types.

  • Fitted by counting labeled PRMBench-V cases, with no EM, so it is supervised naive Bayes despite the Dawid-Skene framing.
  • Best-of-N on MMK12: up to +4.8 points over averaging the judge's scores.
  • Each type has its own label, so it never weights several views of one shared label, as L-SML does.
  • Fair analogue on PRMBench: supervised naive Bayes, label-free Dawid-Skene EM and L-SML on the same binned channels, all flagged by GAP. Not run.

Leaderboards

PRMBench, PRMScore

Socratic-PRMBench, official metric

On Socratic, L-SML loses to its own PRM for 4 of 5 PRMs (see the status report). Prompted critics lead this benchmark.

The PRMBench leaderboard rows come from each paper. Ours use a port of the official evaluator, and our rows use the GAP flag rule, while the leaderboard PRMs use their native thresholds. Under GAP the plain Qwen-72B PRM already reaches 69.5, so part of the gap to the leaderboard is the rule. The fair comparison is L-SML against its own PRM under the same rule: +1.6 to +6.9 points.

Secondary comparisons

  • ARES (Macro-F1, threshold chosen by 5-fold CV on labels; full PRMBench, approximate). On Qwen-7B the PRM and L-SML tie: 68.17 vs 68.10. L-SML is ahead on the weaker PRMs (RLHFlow 64.13 vs 55.79, Math-Shepherd 64.34 vs 53.33). ARES's own appendix puts the plain PRM at .749 against ARES's .736, a difference within one standard deviation by ARES's own report.
  • SLIDER (redundancy class only; its protocol is unclear). On Qwen-7B with GAP, L-SML reaches 71.2 balanced accuracy, against 54.7 for the PRM under the same rule and SLIDER's reported 61.9. With a label-tuned threshold, the PRM has the higher plain accuracy (78.8 vs 74.1), both below the 86.8 of always predicting "correct". L-SML keeps the higher balanced accuracy (74.3 vs 54.5).
  • Hard2Verify. GAP always flags a step, so it cannot score "no error" answers. Error-localization F1 (VerifySteer's metric) needs a rule that can abstain.

What we can and cannot claim

Supported

  • On PRMBench, label-free fusion improves every one of 5 PRMs under the same rule (+1.6 to +6.9 PRMScore).
  • Without retraining or labels, the same PRM reaches PRISM's PRMScore. The flag rule supplies that level; fusion adds 2.4 points under the same rule.
  • On CROP's setting (Qwen-7B), label-free fusion matches their supervised Trace+PRM (22.3% vs 21.8% kept, inside the seed spread). A format/position + PRM LR comes close to their row, which suggests that much of their gain over the PRM is available from surface cues.
  • Step-level label-free fusion of a PRM with its own telemetry is, as far as we searched, new. The estimator family (FUSE, CARE) is not.

Not supported

  • Beating PRISM: at PRISM's operating point, fusion and the plain PRM tie.
  • Beating CROP: the kept-prefix margin is a quarter of the seed SD, and its pooled AUROC is higher (.834 vs .792).
  • Beating supervised fusion: an LR on our own telemetry is better.
  • A new ensembling estimator: L-SML, FUSE and CARE share the same moment-based family.
  • General transfer: on Socratic and Hard2Verify the fused score helps weak PRMs and hurts strong ones.
  • A guarantee: our score is not calibrated across benchmarks.
  • Untouched confirmation: all PRMBench results are development data.

Open before a paper

  • Baselines reviewers will ask for, all on CPU from existing scores: FUSE at the step level, CARE on our channel banks, and a BR2-style naive Bayes / Dawid-Skene EM analogue.
  • Mind the Gap's entropy-drop signal, as a 17th channel and alone on PRMBench.
  • Report PRMScore next to a constant-k reference, so the GAP rule's prior is visible.
  • The cleanest test of the fusion step against CROP: run our label-free fusion on CROP's own Llama-3.1-8B features. This needs new GPU inference.
  • Five of CROP's 5,000 traces (duplicates) have no scores. We report 4,995.
  • Optimal Aggregation would need Best-of-N generation. Weaver has no step-level results to compare against.
