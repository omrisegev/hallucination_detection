Label-free step-error localization: findings for the advisor meeting

The task: find the wrong steps in a given answer, with no labels and no training. We do one teacher-forced pass with Qwen3-4B or Qwen3-8B. We read token-probability channels for each step. Then we fuse the channels without labels. PRMBench and ProcessBench are development data; Hard2Verify and Socratic are exploratory transfer. Banks: N11 < N13 < N22 < N40 < N53; digit twins N14 < N16 < N25 < N43 < N56.

Q1 · Fusion vs plain averageFusion saturates. On PRMBench, all label-free rules give almost the same score. Our pipeline ties the DS-signed average (15 better, 15 worse, of 36 method × bank pairs). On ProcessBench, the plain average (SLA 0.414, N16) is better than all four pipeline variants (0.396–0.408).
Q2 · CompetitorsPRMBench: our top step is an error more often than the top step of the supervised PRM-7B. But the PRM has a higher within-answer AUC (0.80 vs 0.79). ProcessBench: we are above our run of Mind the Gap in all 8 cells. Against the paper's numbers, the result is mixed. We are far below the PRM (SLA 0.57, its top step).
Q3 · Practical valueThe ranking works. It needs no labels and only one pass. On two error types, redundancy and circular logic, we rank errors better than the PRM. Our weak point: the decision that an answer is clean (ProcessBench F1 about 0.40; PRM 0.73).
HeadroomA supervised LR on the same channels adds +0.03 to +0.07 AUC on PRMBench. On ProcessBench it adds almost nothing. Thus the channels (the representation) set the score, not the fusion rule.

Methods and metrics

All methods see the same z-scored step channels of one bank. Each method fits on one cell (one benchmark subset × one scoring model). No method uses labels, except the LR reference. The methods differ only in how they weight the channels.

Signals: teacher forcing, written-token surprise, varentropy

Teacher forcing (deep dive). The scoring model reads a given answer in one forward pass. It generates nothing. The input is the Qwen3 chat template: our math prompt, the question and " /no_think" in the user turn, then the answer steps (joined by blank lines) in the assistant turn. At each answer token we keep the top-50 log-probs and the log-prob of the written token. Mind the Gap scores ProcessBench in the same way, with the same Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B. The term comes from Williams & Zipser (1989).

How Mind the Gap works deep dive →

  1. Evidence per token: Ê = −H(P̃). P̃ = the top-20 probabilities, renormalized.
  2. Smooth Ê with an EMA (span 5).
  3. Flux: Δ = Ẽj − Ẽj−1. A large negative Δ is a drop.
  4. Answer score: mean of the 5 largest drops. Step level: the first step where Δ crosses a threshold. The paper does not define this threshold or the token-to-step map.

Our reproduction: 572 settings × 8 cells. The closest setting gives 37.00 vs 39.27 in the paper, but only when answers where the detector never fires are left out. With all wrong answers, the same setting gives 23.92. The released code has no step-level code and uses another signal (18.42 points off). Our comparator: mean of the 5 largest drops in the step, top step, all wrong answers (SLA 0.332).

Single channels vs fusion

What the tail-vote pipeline taught us (last three weeks) deep dive →

  • Binary tail votes do not beat continuous fusion: vs IU-PCR on the same banks, the pipeline is clearly worse in 21 and clearly better in 9 of 40 PRMBench comparisons; vs continuous L-SML 26 worse, 11 better.
  • L-SML groups are no better than random groups of the same sizes as stage-2 input (0.784 vs 0.787).
  • Between-group weights are near the supervised optimum (0.782 vs 0.784); the loss is equal averaging inside groups (non-negative supervised weights recover 86–97% of it).
  • DS top-3 pruning and the TCI triplet rule did not generalize across banks (group results on the September banks).

What the label-free estimators get right

The estimators (DS, MoM) rank the channels well: a channel with high true accuracy gets a high estimate (Spearman 0.73–0.80). We use this order to drop wrong-way channels and to weight groups. But they estimate the error rate 1.5–2.2 times too high (0.21–0.31 vs 0.14), because the channels share information beyond the truth. We do not use that rate.

Teacher forcing vs the model's own answers deep dive →

Teacher forcing scores text another model wrote (off-policy). To check, Qwen3 answered problems itself; two LLM judges (Claude and GPT) labelled the steps independently (κ = 0.95 on error vs no error, 958 answers).

  • Ranking holds: within-answer AUC 0.757 on 388 own answers vs 0.763 on ProcessBench GSM8K+MATH (difference interval −0.042 to +0.028; equivalence not shown).
  • The exact first-error hit drops: 26% (417 answers) vs 44% (ProcessBench GSM8K+MATH).

Caveats

Teacher-forcing prompt format (found while preparing this meeting). Our teacher-forced input lacks Qwen3's empty think block (<think></think>) that the model writes in /no_think mode. The first answer token gets 34–36 nats of surprise and the raw step surprise peaks at step 0 in 90–93% of ProcessBench GSM8K/MATH answers (true first error at step 0: 18–19%). Effect on final metrics unknown. Check: CPU re-score with the first tokens masked; fix: GPU re-score with the block (needs approval). Own-answer telemetry is not affected, so the own-answers comparison crosses telemetry formats.
  • Written-token surprise is built from published parts (SelfCheckGPT −log p; Fast-DetectGPT-style z; Mind the Gap's drop rule, which already has an "LN-S Drop" column). Ours: the step readouts and their use inside label-free fusion.
  • N16 was chosen after seeing results; all PRMBench and ProcessBench numbers are development data.
