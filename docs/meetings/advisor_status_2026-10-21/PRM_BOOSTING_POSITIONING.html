PRM Boosting against the closest methods

Our method fuses a frozen PRM with telemetry from the same forward pass, at the step level and without labels. Against the two papers on the same PRM and benchmark, it matches CROP's supervised proxy (22.3% vs 21.8% of each trace kept, inside the seed spread) and reaches PRISM's PRMScore without retraining. Most of the PRISM-level score comes from our flag rule; fusion adds 2.4 points on top of it. The estimator family is not new: FUSE (ICML 2026) uses the same Nadler line to ensemble verifiers, but only to select whole responses. As far as we have searched, no paper fuses at the step level.

The methods, one by one

What each paper sets out to solve, the mathematics it rests on, what it actually runs, and how it evaluates itself. Checked claim by claim against the original PDFs on 24 October 2026.

PRISM

arXiv 2606.09078, June 2026 · retrains the PRM
Problem
PRMs over-credit wrong steps: Qwen-PRM-7B passes about 69% of erroneous PRMBench steps. Step labels lean to "correct" (73.1% of PRM800K steps, against 14.2% of full solutions).
Basis
A Bradley-Terry pairwise loss, −log σ(r⁺ − r⁻), on a correct and a wrong step after the same prefix; it depends only on their margin. A Best-of-N bound: false positives cap accuracy at p / (p + (1−p)α), false negatives only slow convergence (Thm 2.1).
Method
Fine-tunes Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B on PRM800K pairs plus "lookahead" negatives (a later valid step used too early), trained easy to hard.
Evaluation
PRMBench PRMScore, FPR/TPR/TNR and categories; ProcessBench; Best-of-N and RL. The threshold is not stated (the 0.5 points of Fig. 5b match Table 2). A code link is given; the repository is empty and no weights are released.

CROP

arXiv 2605.30085, EMNLP 2026 Oral per arXiv · supervised fusion, conformal threshold
Problem
Wrong solutions usually start correct. How long a prefix can be kept with a guarantee that it is clean?
Basis
Conformal risk control with a monotone binary loss: the largest threshold λ with (1 + calibration failures) / (n + 1) ≤ α. Guarantee: marginal P(returned prefix contains an annotated error) ≤ α under exchangeable whole traces (Prop. 1); not a per-prefix guarantee.
Method
Risk proxies: random, format LR, trace-feature LR, the frozen PRM, and Trace+PRM (an LR on Llama-3.1-8B trace features with the PRM risk as one more feature). 60/20/20 splits; each trace is cut at the first step above λ̂.
Evaluation
Six step-labelled sets, PRMBench (first 5,000) and ProcessBench among them: prefix kept, risk, pooled step AUROC, boundary deviation, repair accuracy.

FUSE

arXiv 2604.18547, ICML 2026 · label-free, whole responses
Problem
Pick the best of N responses from m imperfect verifiers, with no labels.
Basis
A Dawid-Skene latent model and the Jaffe et al. (2015) method of moments: under triplet conditional independence the off-diagonal second moments are rank one and the third moments reveal class balance, giving each verifier's sensitivity and specificity (Thm 2.3).
Method
Per-verifier thresholds chosen to minimize a label-free measure of triplet-independence violation (Prop. 2.4); posteriors averaged over triplets give pseudo-labels; a logistic aggregator is fitted on them.
Evaluation
Best-of-N accuracy on GPQA, MATH500, MMLU(-Pro), HLE and IMO Shortlist, against supervised baselines that get labels for 5% of questions.

CARE

arXiv 2603.00039, ICML 2026 · label-free core, labelled tuning
Problem
LLM judges share confounders (length, style), so their errors are correlated and averaging or Dawid-Skene weight them wrongly.
Basis
The judges' precision matrix splits into a sparse part (direct judge–judge dependence) minus a low-rank part (dependence through quality and the confounders); identifiable up to sign and order under eigengap and orthogonality conditions.
Method
SVD variant (the leading factor is quality) and tensor variant (three conditionally independent judge groups, CP decomposition). Hyperparameters are tuned on a 15% labelled split.
Evaluation
12 judge benchmarks, MAE or accuracy against human ratings; none step-level.

Ising aggregation

arXiv 2601.22336 · label-free, binary votes
Problem
Dependent LLM judges break Dawid-Skene and weighted majority vote.
Basis
Class-conditional Ising models of binary votes with pairwise couplings. Couplings shared by both classes keep the Bayes rule linear; class-dependent couplings make it quadratic. The theorems construct regimes where a rule assuming conditional independence stays suboptimal even with many judges.
Method
EM with pseudo-likelihood on unlabelled items.
Evaluation
Accuracy on three judge tasks (WikiQA, Jigsaw, CNN/DM) with six judges.

Mind the Gap

ICML 2026, PMLR v306 · one telemetry channel, no PRM
Problem
Averaging uncertainty over an answer misses one sharp failure.
Basis
A "reasoning manifold" framing: a transition unseen in training pushes an entropy-regularized model toward uniform, so evidence drops (Thm 3.1). The proof uses top-K probability mass; the algorithm uses negative top-K entropy.
Method
Per token, negative entropy over the top 20 tokens, smoothed; risk = mean of the 5 steepest drops; the first large drop marks the first error. One teacher-forced pass on ProcessBench.
Evaluation
Selective accuracy and AURC on GSM8K and MATH; exact first-error accuracy on ProcessBench (not its F1). Thresholds set on a labelled half of the test data.

BR2-PRM

ACL 2026 (PRMBench-V paper) · supervised, multimodal
Problem
A judge's reliability differs by error type, and a plain average of its nine dimension scores ignores that.
Basis
One latent error variable per type; categorical score likelihoods with Dirichlet priors, fitted by counting known labels (no EM); a posterior per type, multiplied over types assuming independence.
Method
Scores each whole answer and picks the best of N.
Evaluation
PRMBench-V (907 questions × 9 error types, strict accuracy) and Best-of-N on MMK12.

ARES

EMNLP 2025 · training-free scoring, labelled threshold
Problem
A step that follows from an earlier wrong step looks valid: errors propagate.
Basis
A step's soundness is the probability it is entailed by the question and earlier steps, where each earlier step is kept with probability equal to its own entailment score in that sample. A Hoeffding bound (Thm 3.1) sets the number of samples; it guarantees only that the estimate is within ε of this model-defined score, not that the step is correct.
Method
An LLM judge or a PRM as the entailment model; up to hundreds of samples per chain; the threshold is chosen on labels by 5-fold cross-validation.
Evaluation
Macro-F1 on a PRMBench subset (size not stated), DeltaBench, ClaimTrees and CaptainCook4D.

R-PRM

EMNLP 2025 · a trained generative PRM
Problem
Scalar PRMs need much labelled data and give no reasoning.
Basis
A judge writes an analysis of each step and ends with Yes/No; the reward is the mean P(Yes) over K sampled analyses (K = 10 by default).
Method
Fine-tunes Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct: SFT on Llama-3.3-70B critiques filtered by PRM800K labels, then DPO with critiques that agree with the label preferred over those that do not.
Evaluation
ProcessBench F1, PRMBench (66.8 for the DPO model), Best-of-N and guided search.

SLIDER / Step-RRI

arXiv 2610.00571, ICLR 2026 workshop per arXiv · redundancy only
Problem
Long reasoning repeats itself, and mutual-information measures do not separate new information from repeated information.
Basis
Partial information decomposition of what the prefix and the current step say about the final answer into redundant, unique and synergistic parts. Step-RRI = redundancy − η·max(unique, synergy), positive when a step repeats the prefix (Thm 2).
Method
Up to 50 number-changed variants per problem from GPT-4o-mini, MiniLM embeddings, k-means, a convex PID estimator; the threshold comes from a linear classifier trained on a labelled held-out set.
Evaluation
PRMBench redundancy class, "accuracy" (Fig. 7, not defined); correlation with reasoning length; data selection for fine-tuning.

  • Where the gain comes from. With one global threshold the fused score and the PRM trace almost the same curve. The fused score pulls ahead only when each answer is cut at its own largest gap.
  • The rule has a prior built in. GAP always flags at least one step. On the one category of correct solutions (MS), PRMScore falls from 99.87 for the native PRM to 94.64 for the PRM under GAP and 88.87 for L-SML under GAP. On PRMBench, a constant "flag 2 steps" rule scores slightly higher than GAP (+0.09 to +1.18 for L-SML; +0.40 on Qwen-7B), which reflects the benchmark's construction of about two errors per answer. Leaderboard PRMs use native thresholds, so a raw leaderboard comparison mixes the rule with the method.
  • On the other PRMs, at matched TPR, the fused score is ahead even under a global threshold: PURE FPR 49.8 vs 52.2, RLHFlow 57.8 vs 77.1, Math-Shepherd 57.3 vs 81.3. On Qwen-72B it is slightly behind (45.9 vs 45.3).
  • The claim we can make: without retraining and without labels, the same PRM reaches PRISM's PRMScore, and fusion adds 2.4 points over the PRM under the same rule. We should not claim we beat PRISM: at PRISM's own operating point, fusion and the plain PRM tie. PRISM's checkpoint is not public, so we cannot boost it.

CROP: the same idea with labels, matched without them

CROP's Trace+PRM proxy is the closest prior task: a step-level fusion of a PRM with model telemetry. Theirs is a logistic regression trained on step labels from 60% of the traces. We reproduced their protocol on our scores: the first 5,000 PRMBench traces (4,995 scored), their splits, seeds 2806–2815, risk ≤ 5%. CROP searches a fixed grid of 101 thresholds. We report that grid and a finer quantile grid, which keeps a little more for every proxy. We also added controls they do not have.

  • On Qwen-7B, the PRM CROP used, label-free L-SML matches their supervised proxy at the same risk: 22.3% vs 21.8% on CROP's grid, with our fusion fitted only on the training traces. The margin is about a quarter of our seed SD (1.9 points over 10 seeds), so this is a tie, not a win. Fitted on all traces, it is 21.9%. Only our variant with label-chosen channels (24.4%) clears it, and that variant is not label-free.
  • Most of CROP's gain over the PRM may come from surface cues. Our format and position features plus the PRM, with no Llama features, come within 0.6 points of their Trace+PRM on their grid (21.2% vs 21.8%; AUROC .831 vs .834). Our format/position LR alone also matches their token/format row (16.3% vs 16.2%). This is an inference from our reconstruction: we did not ablate CROP's own features.
  • On the other PRMs the picture is mixed. Against the supervised format/position + PRM LR (quantile grid, training-trace fit), L-SML is ahead on Qwen-72B (22.3% vs 21.3%) and behind on PURE (20.7 vs 22.1), RLHFlow (16.4 vs 17.8) and Math-Shepherd (17.4 vs 18.6). The order is the same on CROP's grid. The plain Qwen-72B PRM finds no threshold that meets 5% on any of the 10 seeds (risk 7.4%).
  • Our telemetry is the better input. A supervised LR on it keeps 25.1–25.4% (quantile grid, 13 or 16 channels). The label-free fusion recovers 81–92% of that on the Qwen and PURE PRMs.
  • Where CROP is stronger.
    • Its pooled AUROC is higher (.834 vs .792). Our scores are standardized within each answer, a likely reason, since pooled AUROC compares steps across answers.
    • It offers a guarantee within one domain, and ours does not survive a domain shift. Calibrated on PRMBench and tested on Socratic at a 5% target, L-SML reaches 6.7% risk on Qwen-7B and 8.2–25.9% on the other four PRMs, while each plain PRM stays at or below 5%. CROP's own cross-domain diagnostic also breaks (14.8% risk from arithmetic to GSM8K), so both need recalibration under shift.

Methods that share our estimator or our signal

  • FUSE and CARE share our estimator family. Both are moment- or spectrum-based label-free aggregators, close to L-SML, but neither works at the step level. What is new in our work is the level and the views: one answer at a time, step by step, with the views taken from the PRM's own forward pass, scored with official PRMScore.
  • FUSE's finding mirrors ours. The plain Jaffe 2015 weighting loses to a simple average in 7 of 10 of its settings. On our corrected-template PRMBench banks, the plain mean matches or beats telemetry-only L-SML on 7 of 10 banks. FUSE's threshold transform and pseudo-label aggregator, and CARE's dependency graph, are concrete candidates for the open direction of replacing the average.
  • CARE models what we lack. A nuisance shared by a whole family of channels, such as step position, gets its own latent factor instead of being treated as dependence. Its published version tunes hyperparameters on labels and assumes quality is the dominant factor; neither holds for us by default.
  • Ising aggregation gives the theory: rules that assume conditional independence can stay suboptimal when judges are dependent. It covers binary votes only.
  • Mind the Gap uses our access with one channel. Fair test: add its entropy-drop signal as a 17th channel, and run it alone on PRMBench with GAP. Not measured yet.
  • BR2-PRM is a supervised relative. Fair analogue on PRMBench: supervised naive Bayes, label-free Dawid-Skene EM and L-SML on the same binned channels, all flagged by GAP. Not run.

Socratic-PRMBench, official metric

On Socratic, L-SML loses to its own PRM for 4 of 5 PRMs (see the status report). Prompted critics lead this benchmark.

The PRMBench leaderboard rows come from each paper. Ours use a port of the official evaluator, and our rows use the GAP flag rule, while the leaderboard PRMs use their native thresholds. Under GAP the plain Qwen-72B PRM already reaches 69.5, so part of the gap to the leaderboard is the rule. The fair comparison is L-SML against its own PRM under the same rule: +1.6 to +6.9 points.

SLIDER: their Figure 7 on our scores

SLIDER's only PRMBench result is Figure 7: on the redundancy class, a linear classifier trained on a labelled held-out set sets each method's threshold, and "accuracy" is reported (Step-RRI 61.9, cosine similarity 51.6, InfoGain 48.4). The paper does not define the accuracy, the split sizes, the class balance or how step 1 is handled. We ran the same procedure on our scores (758 problems, 13.2% of steps redundant) and report both readings of the metric.

Hard2Verify. GAP always flags a step, so it cannot score "no error" answers. Error-localization F1 (VerifySteer's metric) needs a rule that can abstain.

What we can and cannot claim

Supported

  • On PRMBench, label-free fusion improves every one of 5 PRMs under the same rule (+1.6 to +6.9 PRMScore).
  • Without retraining or labels, the same PRM reaches PRISM's PRMScore. The flag rule supplies that level; fusion adds 2.4 points under the same rule.
  • On CROP's setting (Qwen-7B), label-free fusion matches their supervised Trace+PRM (22.3% vs 21.8% kept, inside the seed spread). A format/position + PRM LR comes close to their row, which suggests that much of their gain over the PRM is available from surface cues.
  • Under ARES's protocol (Macro-F1, CV threshold), L-SML ties the PRM on the two Qwen PRMs and beats it on the other three (+1.2 to +11.0). In ARES's own table, ARES does not beat the PRM it wraps.
  • On PRMBench's redundancy class, under a balanced-accuracy reading of SLIDER's procedure, L-SML reaches 67.6–76.1 with their label-trained threshold and 66.3–73.7 with our label-free GAP rule, against Step-RRI's reported 61.9, with no extra LLM calls. The reading of their metric is an inference.
  • Step-level label-free fusion of a PRM with its own telemetry is, as far as we searched, new. The estimator family (FUSE, CARE) is not.

Not supported

  • Beating PRISM: at PRISM's operating point, fusion and the plain PRM tie.
  • Beating CROP: the kept-prefix margin is a quarter of the seed SD, and its pooled AUROC is higher (.834 vs .792).
  • Beating supervised fusion: an LR on our own telemetry is better.
  • A new ensembling estimator: L-SML, FUSE and CARE share the same moment-based family.
  • General transfer: on Socratic and Hard2Verify the fused score helps weak PRMs and hurts strong ones.
  • A guarantee: our score is not calibrated across benchmarks.
  • Untouched confirmation: all PRMBench results are development data.

Open before a paper

  • Baselines reviewers will ask for, all on CPU from existing scores: FUSE at the step level, CARE on our channel banks, and a BR2-style naive Bayes / Dawid-Skene EM analogue.
  • Mind the Gap's entropy-drop signal, as a 17th channel and alone on PRMBench.
  • Report PRMScore next to a constant-k reference, so the GAP rule's prior is visible.
  • SLIDER bridge: their cosine-similarity baseline under our split, to confirm which accuracy they report.
  • The cleanest test of the fusion step against CROP: run our label-free fusion on CROP's own Llama-3.1-8B features. This needs new GPU inference.
  • Five of CROP's 5,000 traces (duplicates) have no scores. We report 4,995.
  • Optimal Aggregation would need Best-of-N generation. Weaver has no step-level results to compare against.
