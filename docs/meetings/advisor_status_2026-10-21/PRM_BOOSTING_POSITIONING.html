PRM Boosting against the closest methods

Two recent papers define our position: PRISM (retrains the same PRM) and CROP (fuses a PRM with telemetry, with supervision). Against both, label-free PRM boosting matches or exceeds their published numbers on PRMBench. Neither paper reports a label-free step-level fusion of a PRM with telemetry. As far as we have searched, we are the only one.

PRISM: matched without retraining

PRISM retrains the PRM and reports TPR 87.3 / FPR 47.1 / PRMScore 68.0 on PRMBench. Our label-free fusion of the unchanged PRM with the GAP rule lands at TPR 88.6 / FPR 45.1 / PRMScore 70.4. That point is better on both rates, so no threshold choice is needed to compare them.

The improvement appears when the fused score is cut per answer at its largest gap. The PRM cut the same way stays close to its own curve (PRMScore 68.01).
  • On the other PRMs, at matched TPR, the fused score is ahead even under a global threshold: PURE FPR 49.8 vs 52.2, RLHFlow 57.8 vs 77.1, Math-Shepherd 57.3 vs 81.3. On Qwen-72B it is slightly behind (45.9 vs 45.3).
  • The claim we can make: without retraining and without labels, the same PRM plus telemetry reaches and passes PRISM's reported point. PRISM's checkpoint is not public, so we cannot boost it.

CROP: the same idea, done with labels

CROP's Trace+PRM proxy is the closest prior work: a step-level fusion of a PRM with model telemetry. Theirs is a logistic regression trained on step labels from 60% of the traces. We ran their full protocol on our scores: the first 5,000 PRMBench traces, their splits and seeds, risk ≤ 5%. We also added controls they do not have.

  • CROP's gain over the PRM comes from surface cues. Format and position features plus the PRM reproduce their Trace+PRM row (21.7% vs 21.8%, AUROC .831 vs .834), without any Llama features. On PRMBench their model-derived features add nothing measurable.
  • On Qwen-7B, the PRM CROP used, label-free PRM boosting keeps more of each trace than their supervised proxy (23.2% vs 21.8%) at the same risk, with our fusion fitted only on the training traces. On CROP's own fixed threshold grid the margin shrinks to a tie (21.9%).
  • On the other PRMs the picture is mixed. Against the supervised format/position + PRM LR, PRM boosting is ahead on Qwen-72B (22.0% vs 21.3%) and behind on PURE (20.7 vs 22.1), RLHFlow (16.3 vs 17.8) and Math-Shepherd (17.3 vs 18.6).
  • Our telemetry is the better input. A supervised LR on it keeps 25.1%. The label-free fusion recovers 82–93% of that on the Qwen and PURE PRMs.
  • Where CROP is stronger.
    • Its pooled AUROC is higher. Our scores are standardized within each answer, which hurts pooling across answers.
    • It offers a guarantee. When we calibrate on PRMBench and test on Socratic, our fused score breaks it: 6.7% risk at a 5% target.

Leaderboards

PRMBench, PRMScore

Socratic-PRMBench, official metric

On Socratic, PRM boosting loses to its own PRM for 4 of 5 PRMs (see the status report). Prompted critics lead this benchmark.

The PRMBench leaderboard rows come from each paper. Ours use the same official evaluator, but our rows use the GAP flag rule, while the leaderboard PRMs use their native thresholds. Under GAP the plain Qwen-72B PRM already reaches 69.5, so part of the gap to the leaderboard is the rule. The fair comparison is PRM boosting against its own PRM under the same rule: +1.6 to +6.9 points.

Secondary comparisons

  • ARES (Macro-F1, threshold chosen by 5-fold CV on labels; full PRMBench, approximate). On Qwen-7B the PRM and PRM boosting tie: 68.17 vs 68.10. PRM boosting is ahead on the weaker PRMs (RLHFlow 64.13 vs 55.79, Math-Shepherd 64.34 vs 53.33). ARES's own appendix puts the plain PRM at .749 against ARES's .736, within one fold SD.
  • SLIDER (redundancy class only; its protocol is unclear). With GAP, PRM boosting reaches 71.2 balanced accuracy, against 54.7 for the PRM under the same rule and SLIDER's reported 61.9. With a label-tuned threshold the PRM is higher on the Qwen PRMs.
  • Hard2Verify. GAP always flags a step, so it cannot score "no error" answers. Error-localization F1 (VerifySteer's metric) needs a rule that can abstain.

What we can and cannot claim

Supported

  • On PRMBench, label-free fusion improves every one of 5 PRMs under the same rule (+1.6 to +6.9 PRMScore).
  • Without retraining, the result reaches and passes PRISM's reported operating point.
  • On CROP's setting (Qwen-7B), without labels, it keeps more of each trace than their supervised Trace+PRM; and CROP's own gain over the PRM is explained by surface cues.
  • Step-level label-free fusion of a PRM with telemetry is, as far as we searched, new.

Not supported

  • Beating supervised fusion: an LR on our own telemetry is better.
  • General transfer: on Socratic and Hard2Verify the fused score helps weak PRMs and hurts strong ones.
  • A guarantee: our score is not calibrated across benchmarks.
  • Untouched confirmation: all PRMBench results are development data.

Open before a paper

  • The cleanest test of the fusion step: run our label-free fusion on CROP's own Llama-3.1-8B features. This needs new GPU inference.
  • Five of CROP's 5,000 traces (duplicates) have no scores. We report 4,995.
  • Optimal Aggregation would need Best-of-N generation. FUSE and Weaver have no step-level results to compare against.
