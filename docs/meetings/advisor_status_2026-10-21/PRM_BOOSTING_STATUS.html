Status report · 21 October 2026 · development data

PRM Boosting: where we stand

We fuse a process reward model's step score with telemetry from the same forward pass, without labels. On PRMBench, under the same flag rule, this beats the PRM on all five PRMs we tested. On two external benchmarks it helps weak PRMs and hurts strong ones, and we now understand why.

PRMBench: a consistent gain

Against the same PRM under the same decision rule, PRMScore rises by +1.6 to +6.9 points on all 5 PRMs, with every 95% interval above zero. Within-answer AUC, which needs no decision rule, rises by +0.037 to +0.112.

External sets: conditional

On Socratic-PRMBench and Hard2Verify the fused score helps the weak PRMs (RLHFlow) and loses to the strong ones (PURE on both; Qwen-7B on Socratic).

The reason

Label-free weights are estimated from correlations between views. A strong PRM that correlates weakly with weak telemetry gets only 4–21% of the weight, so the fusion cannot tell that it is the best view.

The method in one line

PRM step scoreA trained PRM (e.g. Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B) scores each step of a given answer.
+
Telemetry16 channels (entropy, surprisal, logprob margin, energy, top-k turnover, …) from the same teacher-forced pass of the PRM's backbone.
→
Label-free fusionPer-answer standardization, then L-SML (a spectral meta-learner with dependent-group detection). No labels.
→
Flag countGAP: flag the steps above the largest gap in the answer's sorted scores (at most half the steps).

The fusion uses no labels. The PRMs themselves were trained with labels by their authors, and the boosted score inherits that. GAP replaced a fixed "flag the top 20%" rule (Q80) on 21 October. GAP uses no labels, but its cap (at most half the steps) was set knowing that PRMBench has about two errors per answer, and it does not beat a constant "flag 2 steps" (−0.35 [−0.72, +0.00] for PRM boosting on Qwen-7B). Where the PRMBench gain comes from

Three effects add up. First, the flag rule: GAP adds +2.0 to +2.3 points to PRM boosting but only +0.5 to +2.9 to the PRM alone (+0.7 on Qwen-7B, +0.5 on Qwen-72B). The advantage over the PRM therefore depends on the rule. Under the old Q80 rule, boosting is level with the PRM on Qwen-72B (−0.14 [−0.72, +0.45]) and ahead on the other four. Under the same GAP rule, adding the telemetry at equal weight gives most of the gain on the weak PRMs. Learning the weights with L-SML adds a positive part on all five, and on Qwen-72B it is the whole gain.

Is it just step position or length?

  • Position alone gives within-answer AUC 0.662, and step length alone 0.619. That is below the fused scores (0.77–0.84) but not below every PRM: position alone ties RLHFlow's PRM (0.662). A plain average of the PRM with position and length reaches 0.743–0.808.
  • After removing position and length from every channel, PRM boosting still beats the PRM on within-AUC for all 5 PRMs: 0.817 / 0.827 / 0.788 / 0.765 / 0.712 against 0.801 / 0.795 / 0.763 / 0.662 / 0.684.
  • On the three strong PRMs this costs 0.016–0.027. On Math-Shepherd it costs 0.066 and falls below the PRM + position + length average (0.712 vs 0.743), so its digit-channel gain is mostly position and length. These controls remove position and length from the telemetry channels only, not from the PRM.
  • Moving the telemetry to a different answer with the same number of steps destroys the gain.

What we learned about the fusion

1. The PRM is under-weighted when the telemetry is weak

L-SML estimates each view's weight from its covariance with the other views, so a view that correlates weakly with the rest is read as weak. On PRMBench the telemetry is strong, and the PRM gets 13–24% of the weight. On Socratic the telemetry is weak. The strong PRMs, which are the best view there, get only 4–21% of the weight. None of the weighting rules we tested fixes this: signed, absolute and non-negative rank-one weights, with the PRM in three different places in the fusion. In every case the PRM got 0–21%.

2. A sign rule inverted five external fits

On Math-Shepherd (13-channel bank), step position pushes the PRM and the telemetry in opposite directions inside each class. Their covariance is −0.176: −0.186 within class plus only +0.010 of label signal. The spectral learner then reads one view as inverted and gives the PRM a negative weight. A final sign check that makes the score agree with the PRM flips everything, so the fused score becomes minus the telemetry. This happened in 5 of 20 Socratic fits (3 distinct PRM × bank cells) and in none on PRMBench or Hard2Verify. The L-SML paper assumes every view beats random, so non-negative weights are its own assumption. Enforcing them removes the inversion but not the under-weighting.

3. Smaller results

  • Telemetry from a separate model (Qwen3-8B, corrected template), without a PRM: the plain mean equals or beats L-SML on 7 of 10 channel banks, N16 included (within-AUC 0.7811 vs 0.7792). With each PRM's own backbone telemetry, still without the PRM score, the opposite holds: L-SML beats the plain mean on all 5 PRMs (+0.6 to +1.6 PRMScore).
  • The SML weight estimator shrinks weights toward equal. An unbiased rank-one fit does worse on our banks, so the shrinkage acts as useful regularization.
  • The flag count still matters. With the true error count (a label-using ceiling), PRM boosting would reach 75.4 on Qwen-7B, against 70.4 with GAP and 70.8 with a constant "flag 2", which reflects PRMBench's construction of about two errors per answer.

Limits of these numbers

  • Development data. PRMBench has been inspected many times, and so have the external sets. Nothing here is an untouched confirmation.
  • Label access. The fusion is label-free, but the PRMs were trained with labels. A supervised logistic regression on the same inputs is still better: on CROP's protocol (quantile grid) it keeps 25.1–25.4% of each trace against 23.2–23.3% for PRM boosting.
  • The PRM reference uses whichever of its two scales scores better, chosen after scoring. This favours the PRM.
  • GAP never flags zero steps. It cannot say "no error". This is harmless on PRMBench (97% of the scored answers contain an error) but matters on sets with many clean answers.
  • Where labels touched the choices. The 16-channel bank (N16) was kept because it was the best bank on development labels. The GAP cap was set knowing PRMBench's error structure. Math-Shepherd numbers use the corrected digit channels (fixed 19 October).

Proposed next steps

  • Find a label-free way to give a strong PRM its weight. One option is to estimate the weights after removing known nuisances such as step position.
  • Make the sign-safe fusion the default, and pre-register it before any new external test.
  • Improve the within-answer ranking alongside the flag count: the count ceiling (75.4) is still 5 points above GAP.
