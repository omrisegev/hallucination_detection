Internal layers: more measurements, not yet a gain

Internal model layers gave another way to measure uncertainty. They roughly matched the final-output score, but need more access and new-data validation.

Why I tried this. Following Amir's comment about the small feature pool, Bracha suggested either generating several answers or reading internal layers. I tested both; the layer experiment still uses one answer per question.
Status: PRELIMINARY / VALIDATION BLOCKED. The corrected live-capture check and a small architecture-fidelity pilot are unfinished. The comparisons below are retrospective.

1. What “white-box” means here

Gray-box access reads only the final token probabilities produced during generation. White-box access also reads the hidden state inside each transformer layer and projects it through the model's own output head. This projection, called a logit lens, asks what token distribution an intermediate layer would produce:

pℓ,t(v) = softmax(Wout LN(hℓ,t))v;   Dℓ,t = −log pℓ,t(yt);   swhite = Xdepthw h is the hidden state, LN is the model's normalization, Wout is the output head, and D is the surprise assigned to the generated token. Xdepth holds 13 summaries across early, middle and late layers; U-PCR derives w from their covariance without correctness labels.

Several generated answers

As I mentioned on August 3, repeated answers at the same temperature helped one MATH-500 experiment: 0.851 → 0.912 AUROC. Mixing temperatures hurt. I kept this outside the main comparison because it needs several answers per question.

Intermediate layers

I summarized average and unusually large token surprises across early, middle and late depth. I selected the final 13 summaries after inspecting earlier outcomes; only the final U-PCR covariance fit is label-free.

2. What happened

The comparison below is retrospective and uses 31,440 identical answers from 13 dataset–model pairs; one pair means one dataset tested with one model. AUROC measures how well a score ranks wrong above correct answers; 0.5 is random. Neither method has been tested on untouched data. The gray-box comparison is the retrospectively selected mixed-feature DUFS-LIU score: it learns answer similarity from 30 final-probability summaries and smooths the covariance score over that graph.

White minus gray: -0.13 percentage points AUROC, 95% interval [-1.69, +1.23]. The interval includes zero. Hallucination AUPRC (precision–recall area) is 0.6770 white versus 0.6877 gray. Combined score minus gray: +0.72 percentage points, 95% interval [+0.01, +1.41].

My current reading: internal layers recovered nearly the same ranking through a different route, but required stronger access and did not beat final probabilities. This is why I paused. The combined score is only a hypothesis because I chose it after seeing these results.

What I would like to discuss

  • Is similar accuracy from a different measurement route useful enough to justify the extra access?
  • Is the combined score worth one test fixed before opening new labels, or should we stop here?
