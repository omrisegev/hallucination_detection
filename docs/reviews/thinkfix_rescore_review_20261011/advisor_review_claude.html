01

What does PRMBench ask us to detect?

Rank and judge individual steps in supplied mathematical solutions. Its errors cover logical simplicity, soundness and sensitivity—not only a wrong final answer. We extract token-level signals in a teacher-forced forward pass, aggregate them into step channels, and fuse them without fitting to error labels.

Simplicity

1,516 answers

Redundancy and circularity. A step can be unhelpful even when the final answer remains correct.

Soundness

3,029 answers

Confidence, counterfactuals, domain inconsistency and step contradiction.

Sensitivity

1,666 answers

Missing conditions, deception and multiple valid solutions. The last subtype needs valid-step judgment.

Ranking question

Does an annotated error receive a higher risk score than a correct step in the same answer? Mean within-answer AUROC uses 6,030 answers with both kinds of step.

Detection question

Which steps should be flagged, and can the method accept a clean answer? PRMScore pools valid/error F1 on the 6,211 noncontrol answers. Clean controls are checked separately.

02

Put the established methods first

Continuous L-SML

Estimate dependent groups of channels, build a continuous score within each group, then fuse those group scores. Keep the continuous signal throughout.

Conceptual diagram, not a fitted N16 grouping. Native sign; continuous grouping and fusion; no added tail binarization or external filtering. This is the implemented continuous extension, not a theorem for arbitrary continuous channels.

IU-PCR

Estimate feature–target covariance from the observed channel covariance under its moment assumptions. Form a weighted score in the two-component covariance subspace.

Conceptual diagram. Frozen two-component L2 configuration, scale ratio 0.25; native orientation; no exclusion, difficulty gate or average fallback. Assumptions and settings matter.

Fair comparison: the same step channels, same bank and cell fitting scope. Plain average and DS-filter average are controls. Four newer tail-pipeline variants test whether extra filtering, clustering and merging help; they do not define the main story.

Frozen October 7 comparison protocol · Exact corrected implementations. “Label-free fitting” does not make the development-selected bank or presentation held out.

03

The overall comparison: useful signal, modest fusion gains

L-SML and IU-PCR improve on a plain average

N16 AUROC increases from 0.7708 to 0.7792 / 0.7805: descriptive gains of +0.0084 / +0.0097. DS-filter average reaches 0.7801, so neither complex method has established an overall advantage over that control.

PRMScore and ranking answer different questions

Our Q80 pooled scores are close to the PRM reference, although the reported PRM ranking AUC is higher. Different flag thresholds, error-type mixtures and pooling can produce this pattern. Close point estimates do not establish equivalence.

The project has a label-free ranking signal worth studying. The new tail-pipeline variants do not establish a better N16 fusion method: their AUC range is 0.7767–0.7789.

מסר: השיטות הרציפות נותנות אות ברור, אבל התוספת של הפייפליינים החדשים לא משפרת את התמונה ב־N16. ההשוואה החשובה היא בין השיטות הקיימות, הממוצעים וה־PRM, ולא מי ניצח באחד הווריאנטים החדשים.

05

The comparison changes with the kind of error

Simplicity is the strongest complementary signal

Both continuous methods outperform the reported PRM on redundancy and circularity. Those labels include unnecessary reasoning that can preserve final-answer correctness. This is a candidate contribution for the project.

The advantage needs controls

The PRM is stronger on several soundness and sensitivity types. Repetition, step position or length may explain some of our simplicity signal. Test these explanations before claiming a general quality detector.

Ranking success does not solve abstention: global Q80 falsely flags at least one step in 99.5% / 99.3% of synthetic correct answers for L-SML / IU-PCR.

Multiple valid solutions: valid-step F1 is 0.8840 / 0.8859 for L-SML / IU-PCR. There are no error steps in that subtype, so its AUC is undefined. It remains part of the full detection taxonomy; the two-subtype Sensitivity plot above is explicitly partial. See the appendix for aggregation details.

What does this mean for the project?

Supported on the current development data

Continuous L-SML and IU-PCR rank errors above correct steps. Their simplicity signal complements the PRM. Added pipeline complexity has not established a better N16 method, and supervised LR leaves ranking headroom.

Still required for the intended claim

Freeze the comparisons, validate on untouched source groups, control position and repetition, and separate localization from accepting a clean answer. External target refitting is useful exploration but does not complete source-only transfer validation.

Appendix

A1 · Does masking help, and why consider it?

Masking drops the first content token of the answer before step-channel extraction, with aligned spans. It is distinct from repairing the missing empty think block. The rationale is to test whether a prompt-boundary effect contaminates telemetry; the token can also contain useful error signal.

For N16: yes, it slightly hurts both primary methods. Continuous L-SML changes by −0.00075; IU-PCR by −0.00013 AUROC. Across all 10 banks the picture differs: L-SML averages −0.00085 (4 improve / 6 worsen), IU-PCR +0.00480 (8 improve / 2 worsen).

Interpretation: masking is an optional preprocessing ablation, not a required consequence of the think-prefix fix or a demonstrated universal improvement. Choose the preprocessing contract before confirmation; do not select it by the highest development score.

The large old → new score drop mainly belongs to prefix repair, not masking. Original extraction diagnostics suggested a first-token boundary mismatch, but did not prove that every first content token is irrelevant. Main panels retain the campaign primary mask1 input transparently; no protocol promotion was made.

A2 · Review, flag rules and reproducibility

The central corrected N16 score arithmetic passes independent checks. Raw telemetry replay and full end-to-end validation remain unavailable from the published artifacts. The code review still flags false equivalence wording, supervised leakage in restricted LR feature selection, stale-resume risk, an old-input entropy gate and incomplete Sensitivity reporting.

Q80 is one global 80th percentile over answer-normalized steps; it is not a fixed 20% quota per answer. TOP1/TOP2 force flags and are diagnostics. Noncontrol pooled PRMScore includes multi-solutions; the partial Sensitivity chart does not. No full official leaderboard aggregation parity is claimed.
