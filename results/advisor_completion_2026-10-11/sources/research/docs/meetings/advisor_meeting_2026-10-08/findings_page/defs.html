Methods and metrics

All methods see the same z-scored step channels of one bank. Each method fits on one cell (one benchmark subset × one scoring model). No method uses labels, except the LR reference. The methods differ only in how they weight the channels.

Signals: teacher forcing, written-token surprise, varentropy

Teacher forcing. The scoring model reads a given answer in one forward pass. It generates nothing. The input is the Qwen3 chat template: our math prompt, the question and " /no_think" in the user turn, then the answer steps (joined by blank lines) in the assistant turn. At each answer token we keep the top-50 log-probs and the log-prob of the written token. Mind the Gap scores ProcessBench in the same way, with the same Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B. The term comes from Williams & Zipser (1989).

Provenance: we did not invent these parts. Surprise: SelfCheckGPT (2023). Z-test: the Fast-DetectGPT statistic (2024), here per step; per token it is Min-K%++ (2025). Rise: the drop rule of Mind the Gap (2026); their Table 3 also has an "LN-S Drop" column on the written token. Our part: the step readouts and their use inside label-free fusion.

Narrow claim (about 80% confidence): the first label-based evaluation of varentropy for locating the wrong step on process benchmarks. Alone, varentropy channels reach 0.73–0.75 PRMBench AUC (best 0.753, above entropy 0.730). But they correlate 0.91–0.94 with the uncertainty level.
