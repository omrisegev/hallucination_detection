All results in one table

Every method, arm and configuration measured across the five experiments, against three reference points.

In short

  • 82 measured results across 5 experiments, in one sortable table.
  • The bar that matters is 0.7524 - the best method that picks its own measurements without answer keys. GOOD_6 (0.7594) was chosen using answer keys, so it is an anchor, not a fair target.
  • Best non-reference result in the study: 0.7555 (+0.31 pp vs that bar).

How to read the comparison columns

Three reference points, because they answer different questions:

  • vs all measurements (0.7457) — what our detector gets with no selection at all. Anything below this is worse than doing nothing.
  • vs best automatic picker (0.7524) — the project's best method that chooses its own measurements with no answer keys (a6.pl_dufs, pseudo-label gated DUFS). This is the bar a new automatic method actually has to clear.
  • vs six hand-picked (0.7594) — the GOOD_6 subset, chosen once using answer keys and reused everywhere. It is the standing anti-regression anchor, not a fair target for a label-free method.

Not the same thing: an automatic picker chooses measurements itself, per test set, without labels. A fixed subset was chosen once with labels and then reused. The best fixed subset is LOCO_5 at 0.7705 (on 24 of 25 test sets) — higher than GOOD_6 — but it still spent answer keys to be found.

Nothing in this study clears the automatic-picker bar. The best non-reference result anywhere is 0.7555 (triplet method-of-moments + weight by signal over noise + random-matri), which is +0.31 points against 0.7524. Read alongside the win/loss and p columns, not on the average alone.
