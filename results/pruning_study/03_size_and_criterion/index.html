Experiment 3 - Is there a right number of measurements?

Whether accuracy peaks at some middle size, and whether the fit score can find good combinations. Computed fresh on the current 30-measurement pool.

In short

  • The 1.03M-combination cache on disk is unusable - only 3/19 test sets still reproduce (worst disagreement 0.39 AUROC). Everything here is recomputed fresh.
  • No sweet spot. For a typical combination, more measurements is better (25/25 test sets) - so a 'stop where the curve turns' rule has nothing to find.
  • But the best small combinations score far above typical ones: trimming has a high ceiling and a poor average. The value is entirely in choosing well.
  • The fit score barely ranks combinations: mean correlation +0.222, positive in only 23/25 test sets. This does not bear on its use as a localizer (Experiment 2).

  • For a typical combination, keeping more is better. In 25/25 test sets a typical large combination beats a typical small one, and in 22/25 the best typical size is 14 or more.
  • But the best combinations found are small — in 17/25 test sets the best sampled combination had 8 measurements or fewer, and the upper line sits well above the lower one.
  • Together these say: trimming has a high ceiling and a poor average. Cutting measurements at random makes things worse; cutting the right ones makes things much better. All of the value is in choosing well, none of it in being small.
  • Consequence for the algorithm: a rule of the form "trim while the curve improves and stop at the turn" has no turn to find on the typical curve. The size has to be fixed in advance or set by an error-control threshold, not discovered by following a curve.

Question 2 - Does the fit score know which combinations are good?

  • Mean correlation across test sets: +0.222 (median +0.202), positive in 23/25 test sets.
  • Used as a score for ranking whole combinations, the fit score carries little usable information, and its direction is not consistent across test sets, so it cannot be applied with a fixed sign.
  • Limit of this conclusion. This tests one particular use of the fit score: ranking entire combinations against each other. It says nothing about the fit score used as a localizer — deciding which group to repair — which is what Experiment 2 tests. Different quantity, different job.
