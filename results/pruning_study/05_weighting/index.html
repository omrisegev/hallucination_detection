Experiment 5 - How to weight the measurements

Sixteen weighting pipelines as a three-slot factorial, plus a plain-average floor.

In short

  • Current recipe scores 0.7533 on the full 30 measurements.
  • Highest-scoring configuration reaches 0.7555 (+0.22 pp, better on 15/25 test sets).
  • Reported as main effects per slot rather than a winning combination - with 16 configurations and 1.45 points of headroom, best-of-16 is mostly luck.
  • No configuration is being adopted on the strength of a sub-point average.

Why this is a factorial and not a bake-off

Five proposals were put forward for this gap. They are not five rival methods — they sit in three independent slots of one pipeline: how the relationship matrix is cleaned first, how each measurement's reliability is estimated from it, and how those reliabilities become weights. Running them as one flat list would mix the slots together and make it impossible to say which part mattered.

On reading the numbers below. There are 16 configurations, 25 test sets, and only about 1.45 points of headroom in total. The best-looking configuration will look good partly by luck. So the main result is the effect of each slot averaged over the others, and no configuration is called a winner on a small difference in average accuracy.

Reading this

  • Current recipe (leading eigenvector, weight by signal, no conditioning): 0.7533.
  • Highest-scoring configuration: triplet method-of-moments + weight by signal over noise + random-matrix cleaning, at 0.7555 (+0.22 pp, better on 15 of 25 test sets).
  • Whether that difference is real should be judged from the win/loss split and the Wilcoxon column, not the average — and from whether the same slot option also wins in the main-effects chart, where luck has less room to operate.
