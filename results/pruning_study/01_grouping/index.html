Experiment 1 - Does the grouping step help?

Switching off the detector's internal 'group similar measurements' stage, at every subset size, on 25 test sets.

In short

  • At the full 30 measurements, switching grouping off changes accuracy by +1.19 pp (5/6 test sets better without it).
  • Averaged over large subsets (21-30) the difference is +0.81 pp; over small subsets (3-8) it is +0.62 pp - the sign flips with size.
  • The effect is small everywhere. The honest reading is that the grouping step is not earning its keep at full pool size, not that turning it off is a shippable win.

What was compared

Our detector combines measurements in two stages: it first sorts them into groups of measurements that behave alike, combines within each group, then combines the group summaries. This experiment switches that first stage off, so all measurements are combined in one pass, and compares the two at every subset size. Everything else — the data, the scaling, the way the final score's direction is resolved — is identical.

At each size below 30, 20 random combinations of measurements were drawn per test set and the two methods scored on exactly the same combinations. At size 30 there is only one combination, so it is scored directly.

Reading this

  • The grouping step costs accuracy at large sizes and gains at small sizes. Averaged over sizes 21–30 the difference is +0.81 pp in favour of switching grouping off; over sizes 3–8 it is +0.62 pp.
  • The sizes where grouping helps are the sizes where there are too few measurements for a group structure to be worth estimating — and the sizes where it hurts are where the estimate is noisiest.
  • None of these differences is large. They sit in the same range as the spread between neighbouring sizes, so this should be read as "the grouping step is not buying what it was meant to buy at full pool size", not as "switching it off is an improvement worth shipping".
