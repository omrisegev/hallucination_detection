Does clustering find the assumption violation, or just find correlation?

U-PCR study, Phase B2 — redesigned after review exposed the original comparison as a confound

In short

  • Fit error is essentially pair correlation: Spearman 0.870.
  • Raw same-vs-cross ratio 2.03×, but matched on correlation decile it collapses to 0.996.
  • A clustering that ignores L-SML entirely and uses correlation magnitude alone separates the violation 3.41×.
  • PREMISE DOES NOT SURVIVE - the raw effect is a confound. Fit error IS pair correlation (Spearman 0.870); matched on |C_ij| decile the same-vs-cross ratio collapses to 0.980, and a random partition gives 0.989. L-SML membership adds nothing beyond 'these features are correlated', so dropping same-cluster equations has no principled justification. Phase D's failure is the empirical confirmation.

What changed, and why

The first version of this page reported that feature pairs inside a cluster fit the model 2.03× worse than pairs across clusters, in 25 of 25 test sets, and called that strong support for the clustered variant. That was a confound. The fit error is very nearly a restatement of how correlated the pair is (Spearman 0.870), and clustering groups correlated things by definition. So the raw comparison cannot distinguish 'clustering finds the violation' from 'clustering finds correlation'.

The question that actually matters is whether cluster membership predicts violation beyond correlation magnitude. Three controls answer it: matching pairs on their correlation decile, a random partition with identical cluster sizes, and a clustering built on correlation magnitude alone.
