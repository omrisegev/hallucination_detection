Is the component-count threshold the real dial?

U-PCR study, exp07 — lambda2_threshold, the constant that decides one eigenvector or two

In short

  • lambda2_frac is tightly clustered (0.0942–0.2328, median 0.1435) just above the hardcoded 0.1, so the threshold flips many cells at once.
  • Best threshold is 0.25 at +0.43pp over the deployed 0.1.
  • Macro tracks the number of 2-component cells at Spearman -0.831.
  • THE DIAL IS INERT. The best threshold (0.25) buys +0.43pp over the deployed 0.1, inside noise, even though it changes the component count on 24 cells. So the component count is NOT the mechanism behind Step 204's -3.67pp for the 2-eigenvector rule, and Omri's 'the redundant second factor gets in' is not actionable through this knob. Consistent with Step 204's other finding that one-component U-PCR is exactly PC1 of the surviving features - the estimation machinery is inert here and what mattered was orientation and exclusion. AND IT QUALIFIES A STEP-204 HEADLINE: throwing the same switch directly at the DEPLOYED configuration gives -0.43pp mean / +0.07pp median, 15W/9L, p=0.1615 - not the -3.67pp / -2.36pp, 3W/21L, p=9.1e-05 that exp03 reports. exp03's number is a factorial MAIN EFFECT, averaged over the 32 combinations of the other five factors, most of which are not configurations we run. Both are correct measurements of different quantities, but only the deployed-config one answers 'what does the 2-eigenvector rule cost us today', and the answer is: nothing measurable.

What this measures

U-PCR builds its weights from the top one or two eigenvectors of the feature covariance. Which, is decided by a single hardcoded constant: it takes two whenever lambda2 / Trace(C) exceeds lambda2_threshold = 0.1. Step 204 measured the 2-component rule as costing −3.67pp, and this is the knob that sets it. The g2 search range — the suspect this replaced — does not enter the decision at all: the component count is fixed before g2 is fitted (exp01).

The distribution is why the knob is sharp: lambda2_frac runs 0.0942 to 0.2328 with median 0.1435, i.e. it is tightly clustered just above the 0.1 cutoff. Almost every cell is on the same side of it, and a small move in the threshold flips many cells at once.
