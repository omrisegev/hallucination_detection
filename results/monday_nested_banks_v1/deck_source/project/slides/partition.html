Clustering the channels: groups are stable, not independent

Procedure

1. L-SML Algorithm 1 on tie-aware tail marks of the DS survivors; K chosen by the rank-one residual.

2. Merge: ρ(A,B) = λ₂(R[A∪B]) / min(λ₁(R[A]), λ₁(R[B])); merge the smallest-ρ pair while ρ < 0.5.

3. Never below 3 groups; fewer = grouping failure (no fallback).

Origin

Eqs. 14–15 of Jaffe et al. 2016; the merge step is ours (Step 456).

What we know about the groups

Original banks: 0 failures in 72 bank-cells, 3–8 groups. Nested core bank N11: 1 failure (2 groups on one ProcessBench cell).

Found partitions beat 2,000 random ones on dependence, but stay far from conditional independence.

Supervised LR on 30 random equal-size partitions does as well as on ours (0.7874 vs 0.7839 on B13): the partition itself is not validated as useful.

The groups are reproducible and capture real dependence (near-duplicate channels end up together), which is why the group-level estimators behave better than channel-level ones. But a supervised reference does just as well on random groupings of the same sizes, so we cannot claim the clustering adds localization accuracy.
