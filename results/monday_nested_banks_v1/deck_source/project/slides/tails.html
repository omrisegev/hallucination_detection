Why binary tails: estimators need votes, not scores

Per answer, each channel votes +1 ("error") on its top k = max(1, ⌈0.2·n⌉) steps, else −1; exact ties broken by a fixed random key. Votes go to DS, SML, MoM and HEM; continuous values are what we average.

For

  • Dawid–Skene, SML and MoM are defined on ±1 classifiers; no continuous guarantee
  • Less dependence between groups: class-conditional |r| 0.08–0.21 on marks vs 0.14–0.31 continuous
  • Conditional-independence test is far more sensitive on marks (z 13 vs 0.8 for a 0.15-SD shared factor)
  • Stable partition: same 4 groups in 5 of 5 folds (tie-aware marks)

Against / limits

  • Weights learned on marks lose to equal weights: −0.0022 AUC [−0.0045, −0.0001]
  • Prevalence is not estimable: MoM returns the marked share (0.231 vs quota 0.230)
  • Quota hardly matters: 14 thresholds give PRMScore 63.8–64.3
  • Every clean answer also casts votes: false votes by construction

Bottom line: tails are a necessity for the estimators, not a source of accuracy. They make the conditional-independence machinery usable and the partition stable; they do not improve localization by themselves, and the 20% quota is not a tuned parameter (all 14 tested thresholds land within half a PRMScore point).
