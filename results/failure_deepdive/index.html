Why we fail where we fail

Nine cells diagnosed against the other sixteen as the comparison group. Jul-30 advisor action item 1. Diagnosis only — this page names mechanisms and pre-registers repairs; no repair has been run, so none is reported.

0. The obvious answer is a confound, and it is not the answer

The nine weak cells are exactly the nine lowest anchor_auc in the grid, and Spearman(anchor_auc, deployed AUROC) = +0.981. That reads as an orientation failure. It is not one: Spearman(anchor_auc, best single view) = +0.975. The epr anchor is itself a pooled feature, so a weak anchor only means every view is weak on that cell.

Two independent checks agree. The allsigns / z2 / raw / oracle anchor conditions all return identically 0.7594 with zero cells below chance (h1_orientation_summary.csv), and on this page the global-sign rung costs a mean of +0.00pp across all 25 cells. The global orientation bit is being resolved correctly everywhere.

On every one of the 14 healthy cells where signs matter at all, recovery lands between 0.919 and 1.247 (median 1.025) — L-SML gets essentially all of it back, without labels. On the weak cells 4 of 8 fall below 0.9, and no healthy cell does at all. Fisher exact p = 0.0096.

This is the failure. It is not orientation (the global bit is right on 25/25), not the K-selection (§5), and not the pool. It is that the label-free machinery for recovering relative feature polarity stops working on exactly the cells where the views are individually weak — which is where it has the least covariance structure to work from.

So "why do we fail here" has two different answers, and they need different responses. Six cells have a named, fixable defect. Three are simply hard, and on those the honest move is to report the ceiling rather than to chase it.

7. Repairs, pre-registered and NOT tested here

Written down before any of them is run, so the diagnosis cannot be tuned to make a fix look good. Each names the cells it should help and the cells it must not hurt.

  1. A better label-free relative-sign estimator — targets §2b, the headline mechanism, and is the only repair aimed at it. The candidate is Z2 synchronisation on the sign pattern of the correlation matrix (spectral_utils/orientation.z2_sign_recovery, already written and currently unused on this path) in place of L-SML's implicit recovery. Gate: must lift recovery above 0.90 on the four failing cells and move the twenty-two healthy ones by less than 0.5pp.
  2. Rank / quantile transform of each view before fusion — targets the non-monotonicity in §4, which fires on CoQA and nowhere else. Gate: must be a no-op (<0.5pp) on the 24 cells whose nonmono_gain_cv is ≈0, or it is buying CoQA with everything else.
  3. Keep the pool's strongest view unconditionally — targets §3. The selector drops it on four cells, costing up to 4.8pp before fusion even starts. This is label-free only if 'strongest' is decided without labels, which is the hard part and may sink it.

Not proposed, and why: a K-selection change (§5 clears it — 0/25 degenerate, eigengap helps 5/25), anything touching orientation (§0 — the global bit is right on 25/25 and costs 0.00pp), and anything touching pool composition (Step 206 closed it in both directions).
