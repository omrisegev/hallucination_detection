Two ways to combine step signals

Every method on slide 5 scores a step as a weighted sum of its channels. The methods differ only in how they choose the weights.

Channel

One step-level signal, such as entropy, surprise or digit disagreement. A bank (N16, N56) is a set of channels.

Signal of a channel, μ
μᵢ = mean on erroneous steps − mean on correct steps

In standard units. Measuring it requires the step labels.

Redundancy, Σ
Σᵢⱼ = correlation of channels i and j

Measured without labels. Two channels with ρ near 1 repeat each other, including each other's noise.

Voting ruleOUR METHODS
wᵢ ∝ μᵢ

Each channel gets weight for its own signal, judged alone. Plain average, L-SML and IU-PCR estimate these weights without labels.

Conditioning ruleLR
w = Σ⁻¹ μ

Each channel gets weight for what it adds once the other channels are known. A channel that repeats another adds little, so it gets little weight. LR (and LDA) work this way.

Oracle

A reference row that uses the true labels only to compute μ. It is a ceiling for a rule, not a method we could deploy.

Oracle voting = best possible voting. Oracle conditioning = LDA.

Illustration · two channels that repeat each other

Voting

A and its repeat A′ both vote. A's view is counted twice and B's carries half the weight.

Conditioning

Once A is known, A′ adds almost nothing and gets almost no weight. A and B each count once.

The question for the next slides

When we add channels, LR improves and our methods do not. Is that because we estimate the weights badly, or because voting cannot use the new channels at all?

How we test it

Give both rules the true μ. If oracle voting also stays flat, the limit is the rule, not our estimation.

Limit: the illustration is schematic. Negative weights are not the main mechanism: LR restricted to non-negative weights closes 86–94% of the L-SML → LR gap on N14–N56, using about half of the channels. LR's large opposite pairs (e.g. the digit pair at ρ = 0.9998) add only 0.1–0.2 points each.

Draft · why voting gains nothingEffective dimension of each bank

Most added channels repeat channels we already have

From N14 to N56 the bank grows by 42 channels but only by about 3 independent directions.

Effective dimension
(Σλ)² / Σλ² over the eigenvalues λ of Σ

How many independent directions the bank's variation actually spreads over. Adding an exact copy of a channel adds a channel but no direction, and can even lower this number (N43 → N56).

Voting

A repeated channel is counted again, with its noise. At best it changes nothing.

Conditioning

A repeated channel adds almost nothing once its twin is known, so it gets almost no weight. LR with non-negative weights keeps 29 of 56 channels at N56.

Show numbers
Limit: effective dimension is averaged over the five training folds. It describes redundancy and does not by itself show which channels carry signal.

The conditioning share of the gap grows with the bank

At N56 conditioning explains 3.5 of the 5.1 points. Conditioning inside groups of channels gives a large part of it, but random groups give most of that too.

How each bar is built

Estimation = L-SML → oracle voting.
Inside clusters = oracle voting → conditioning applied only within each L-SML cluster.
Between clusters → full oracle conditioning.
Rest → LR.

Inside clusters: 0.2 → 2.1 points

From N16 to N56. Random groups of the same sizes reach 0.787 at N56, against 0.795 with L-SML's clusters. The clusters help by about 0.9 points there and not at all at N16.

N43 is a different failure

Draft · back to slide 16, question 01“Separate feature value, redundancy and score weighting”

What the added channels give, and to whom

The added channels carry little new signal of their own. LR gains by choosing which of the repeated channels to trust, and that choice cannot be read from the correlations without labels.

Feature value

Small. Oracle voting moves by −0.45 points from N14 to N56 (slide G2).

Redundancy

Large. 42 channels add about 3 independent directions (slide G3).

Score weighting

Decisive. Conditioning explains 3.5 of LR's 5.1 points at N56 (slide G4); with non-negative weights it still closes 87%. It cannot be estimated from correlations alone (slide G5).

Questions for the discussion

1. Report the large-bank gap as an identifiability limit of label-free fusion, with the oracle rows as the evidence?

2. Would a small labelled set be acceptable for μ alone, with Σ from all unlabelled steps? This connects to question 02 on label budget. Not yet run.

3. Is there label-free information beyond correlations worth pursuing? Within-answer spikiness points about halfway toward the right direction (cosine ≈ 0.5), but naive optimisation of it fails.
