SUB5: six telemetry channels

  • What it does. Takes six token-level telemetry channels, z-scores each within its answer, groups them label-free into L-SML groups (at least three are required), weights the groups by L-SML (Dawid-Skene EM on per-answer GAP votes), and flags the top steps of each answer with the GAP count rule. Two variants: telemetry only (from the PRM's backbone or from the PRM's standalone base model), and SUB5 + PRM, where the PRM's step score enters as one more channel and anchors the weights.
  • What it measures. q15_H1 (spread of the top-15 next-token distribution), chosen_surprisal (how surprising the written token is), top15_turnover (how much the top-15 candidate set changes between tokens), dominant_freq16 (the dominant frequency of the uncertainty signal over a 16-token window), bocpd_p0 (Bayesian change-point probability) and digit_spread (spread of the probability over digit tokens).
  • Benchmarks. The six channels were chosen out of the 16 with PRMBench and Socratic labels, so those two are in sample; Hard2Verify, ProcessBench, CRV-Arith, CRV-GSM8K, PRM800K and DeltaBench are out of sample. Out of sample, telemetry only beats the PRM in — cells and its own plain average in —; SUB5 + PRM beats the PRM in — and its average in —.

TELE16: all 16 telemetry channels

  • What it does. The same pipeline on the full 16-channel bank: answer z-scores, label-free L-SML groups and weights, GAP readout. Telemetry only comes from the backbone or from the standalone base model. TELE16 + PRM (stored as PRMG_N16) puts the PRM's step score into that same L-SML as one more channel. It differs from CHAIN16, which first fuses the telemetry into group scores and only then combines them with the PRM.
  • What it measures. Uncertainty level: q15_H1, q15_VE1 (top-15 spread measures), chosen_surprisal, logprob_margin (gap between the two most likely tokens), energy_level, true_tail50 (mass outside the top 50). Change over the answer: energy_innovation, top15_turnover, top50_js (divergence between neighbouring top-50 distributions), bocpd_p0, realized_drv. Written token against the distribution: realized_z. Spectral: dominant_freq16. Digits: digit_alternative, digit_spread, digit_alternative_innovation.
  • Benchmarks. The 16-channel bank and the fusion recipe were developed on PRMBench, so PRMBench is in sample; Socratic was run afterwards as an external test and counts as out of sample here, as do the other six. Out of sample, telemetry only beats the PRM in — cells and its plain average (MEAN_N16) in —; TELE16 + PRM beats the PRM in — and its average (EQ_N16) in —.

CHAIN16: the PRM combined with the 16 channels

  • What it does. Two stages, both label-free: L-SML on the 16 channels gives one score per channel group; a second L-SML weighs the PRM's step score against those group scores; GAP flags the top steps (on ProcessBench, the PRM's own gate and count with CHAIN16's order). The telemetry can come from the backbone or from the standalone base model.
  • What it measures. The PRM's step score plus the same 16 telemetry channels as TELE16.
  • Benchmarks. Defined on PRMBench (in sample); every other benchmark is out of sample. Its plain-average control is EQ_N16, the equal-weight mean of the PRM and the 16 channels. Out of sample it beats the PRM in — cells and EQ_N16 in —.

"Beats average, PRM and best published" keeps a cell only when the candidate is above all three at once: its own plain average, the PRM itself under the same rule, and the best published score of any kind for that benchmark and metric. Cells with no published score never pass this filter.

Main metrics: PRMBench PRMScore; Socratic, CRV-Arith, CRV-GSM8K, PRM800K and DeltaBench step macro-F1; Hard2Verify official step balanced F1 (177 answers); ProcessBench average first-error F1. Every candidate and control uses the label-free GAP count rule; on ProcessBench the NC rule (the PRM's native gate and count, the arm's order). "The PRM itself" is that PRM under the same rule (ProcessBench: its native threshold).

Papers: PRMBench (arXiv 2501.03124, Table 3), PRISM (2606.09078), PathFinder-PRM (2505.19706), R-PRM (2503.21295), ActPRM (2504.10559), Socratic-PRMBench (2505.23474, Tables 3-4), Hard2Verify (2510.13744, Table 2), the Qwen PRM paper "The Lessons of Developing PRMs" (2501.07301, Table 7) for ProcessBench, and CROP (2605.30085, Table 6) for kept % and AUROC (its Direct PRM and Trace+PRM use Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B, so they are compared with that PRM only). PRM-specific published rows count only against the same PRM. DeltaBench has no published number on our metric. LLM critics and CROP's supervised rows use labels or many model calls that the candidates do not.

Two cells have no SUB5 score because the six channels form fewer than three L-SML groups: CRV-Arith backbone Qwen-72B (SUB5 + PRM) and CRV-Arith standalone Qwen-7B (SUB5 alone).
