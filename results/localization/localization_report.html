Our label-free arm, put through the protocol of “Mind the Gap: Catching Hallucinations via Evidence Drop” (ICML 2026) — the same metrics, the same calibration, the same baselines — at the answer level and at the step level.

arm: U-PCR over the canonical pool polarity: sign(ρ̂), label-free orientation: cell anchor no subset, no fixed K, no hand-picked views

What is being measured

The paper's contribution is a detector: evidence Ê = −H(P̃) over the renormalized top-20, smoothed with an EMA, and a risk score built from the worst first-differences of that curve — the “drops”. Ours is a different signal fused a different way: a pool of spectral, temporal and log-probability views over the entropy trace, combined by U-PCR with no labels, no hand-picked feature list and no chosen subset size.

Both are one forward pass, both are unsupervised, so they are directly comparable on cost. The question is whether ours also localizes — whether the fused score points at the step where the reasoning broke, not just at the answer being wrong.

Which arm this is, and exactly how far the verification reaches. U-PCR here means upcr_fit over the full canonical pool with the fitted exp06 config and polarity derived from sign(ρ̂). It is not upcr_pipeline and not a fixed subset. An independent audit confirmed it reproduces the maintained scorer bit-for-bit on all 24 in-scope roster cells (max score difference 0.0, identical kept-view counts, matching the recorded auroc_rho_anchor to 5e-4).

The scope of that check, stated precisely: assert_upcr_mirrors_canonical runs on the answer-level cells only. The step-level and token-level arms fit their own detector on a different population and are not covered by it — and the step-level pool is structurally smaller (spectral and time-domain views only; the log-probability and anomaly views have no per-step analogue). Read the step-level numbers with that in mind.

The hand-picked L-SML subsets appear only as clearly-labelled reference rows. The label-free peer to compare against is DUFS + L-SML, which sits within noise of U-PCR across the roster (macro 0.7687 vs 0.7741, 15W/9L of 24, p=0.069) — not the GOOD_* rows.

The step-level pool is smaller than the answer-level one

Two reasons, both structural rather than incidental, and both worth stating before reading any step-level number:

  • Most views need whole-trace context. A step is scored by running the ordinary feature extractor on the slice of the entropy trace belonging to it, which yields the spectral and time-domain views only. The log-probability views and the anomaly views are computed per trace elsewhere in the pipeline and have no per-step analogue, so they are simply absent at step level. That is why the answer-level arm fits on tens of views and the step-level arm on around a dozen.
  • Short steps lose more. compute_spectral_features returns nothing below 8 tokens and compute_stft_features returns 0.0 rather than NaN below 32 — a constant that passes a finiteness check and would enter the fusion as an information-free column. Those views are explicitly marked missing per step instead, so the pool shrinks honestly rather than filling with zeros.

The token trace exists because of the second point: a 32-token sliding window spans step boundaries, so it has no step-length floor at all and keeps the full extractable pool no matter how short the steps are. Where the step arm cannot be fitted, the token arm still can.

Separability of the step-level risk

What is ours and not theirs

The paper defines its risk at the token level but reports localization at the step level, and never says how one becomes the other. Those choices are ours, fixed before any number was computed, and a gap against their table may be a rule difference rather than a method difference:

  • Token → step. A step's risk is the worst first-difference inside it, since the method is explicitly a worst-case drop detector. The mean is computed as a sensitivity, never as the headline.
  • Flux attribution. The difference between tokens j and j+1 belongs to the step containing j+1 — a drop that happens on the way into a bad step belongs to that step.
  • Smoothing is global. One EMA over the whole chain, then partition by step. Smoothing each step separately would reset the filter at every boundary and manufacture a drop there.
  • Rows with no erroneous step are excluded from SLA (there is nothing to localize) but kept for ProcessBench's official F1, which scores exactly that abstention.
  • The step threshold is the α-quantile of per-step risk on annotated erroneous steps in a calibration split — the same Neyman–Pearson construction they use at sequence level. They give no rule at all here.
  • Window and stride. W = 32 tokens is not tuned: it is exactly the minimum length at which the full feature pool is measurable.
