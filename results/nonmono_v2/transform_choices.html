Spectral hallucination detection · transform selection

Folding the axis turns a spread difference into a shift.

A linear fusion can only use a feature whose two class distributions are shifted versions of each other. Several of ours differ in spread instead — correct answers piled in the middle, hallucinations pushed to both tails. No monotone reading separates those. These panels show each candidate transform attempting the conversion, and whether it works.

Omri Segev Moshe · 2 Aug 2026 · 38 candidates, 61 controls

How to read a panel

correct answers hallucinations AUROC is cross-fitted, with the sign chosen on the training folds

AUROC is invariant under any monotone transform. So the raw feature's AUROC is the ceiling over every monotone reading of it, and any option that beats it is necessarily non-monotone. That makes each panel a self-contained test: no fusion, no selector, no ensemble.

The selection rule, fixed before looking: among options that use no labels from any cell, take the highest AUROC, and adopt it only if it beats the raw view by at least 2.0pp. Label-fitted options (|u−c*|, hinge, the LOCO fits) are computed and shown as diagnostics — they mark the family's own ceiling — but they are never chosen.

What each option buys

Candidates are views with at least 3.0pp of measured non-monotone headroom. Controls are the rest — views already read correctly, where the same transforms should and do hurt.

The controls column is the honest test. A transform that gains on candidates and also gains on controls is not detecting anything — it is just adding variance the AUROC estimate happens to reward.

Can we tell which views to fold, without labels?

This is the binding constraint. Folding a view that is already monotone costs several points, so applying it everywhere is worse than doing nothing. Step 217 established that the marginal shape cannot decide it — whether the feature's own histogram has two humps is uncorrelated with whether its label curve bends.

The candidate here builds a pseudo-label from the other views (their simple average, which needs no answer key) and asks whether the view bends against that. Measured across all 99 views:

Spearman(consensus-detected bend, true headroom) = +0.309, p = 1.88e-03, n = 99

Of the 61 control views, the label-free rule would still transform 13. Those are the false positives any deployable rule has to pay for, and they are counted against it, not hidden.

Ordered by how much headroom exists. Each card shows every option, the chosen one outlined, and underneath, where the fold's centre should sit — scored against the true labels and against the label-free consensus, so you can see whether the right centre is recoverable without an answer key.

Controls — where the transforms should fail

Six views with no measured headroom, run through the identical pipeline. The two class densities here are already shifted rather than differently spread, so folding the axis destroys a working feature. Adopted on 34 of 38 candidates; on these, the rule should and mostly does keep the raw view.
